Soviet **5.45×39mm** are rimless bottlenecked rifle cartridges developed near the end of the 1960s for use with the new [Avtomat Kalashnikova Model 1974](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/wikipedia:AK-74). These are the standard cartridge for almost every combustion-based firearm found in the [Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Apocalyptic_Metro_System), be it an [assault rifle](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kalash_%28AK-74M%29), a [carbine](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bastard), or a [sniper rifle](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/VSV).

Unlike pre-war [military grade rounds](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/military_grade_rounds), dirty 5.45mm rounds were produced post-war in the Metro or other surviving settlements, most likely by refilling empty 5.45mm casings using custom-made, lower-quality propellant. Because dirty rounds can be easily reproduced and are very common, they have almost no value and cannot be used as currency. 

Dirty 5.45mm rounds are one of the most common ammo types used throughout all three Metro games, because many widespread and versatile weapons, such as [Kalash](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kalash), [VSV](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/VSV), or even metro-made weapons, such as the [Bastard](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bastard), use this type of ammunition. The standardization of the 5.45×39mm cartridge even in post-war made weaponry implies that Metro technicians and gunsmiths modified weapons in order to ease the strain on resources and labor. Dirty rounds, however, have less stopping power than military grade rounds, possibly due to the use of lower-grade homemade propellant which gives the bullets less kinetic energy.

### Metro 2033
Dirty 5.45×39mm rounds are some of the most common types of [ammunition](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/ammunition) in *[Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_%28Videogame%29)*. They can usually be found in many places, including dead bodies, in crates, or in hideouts and friendly stations. The [armorer](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Pavel_%28Exhibition%29) in [Exhibition](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Exhibition_%28Location%29) will give Artyom a batch of one hundred dirty 5.45 cartridges, and ten military grade rounds, along with his first [Bastard](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bastard) carbine. They can also be purchased at ammo vendors. One MGR can buy five dirty rounds, while 10 dirty rounds can be sold for 1 MGR.

### Metro: Last Light
Dirty rounds return in *[Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro:_Last_Light)* and are still very common, although their value has increased slightly, as one MGR is now worth only three rounds. The arsenal of weapons that use this type of ammo has been expanded and now includes, for example, the [RPK](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/RPK) or the [AKS-74U](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Modified_Russian_Rifle_%28AKS-74U%29). Previously, Artyom could carry as many rounds as needed, now, he cannot carry more than 360 dirty 5.45mm rounds, although the capacity of MGRs remains infinite.

### Metro Exodus
5.45mm rounds make another appearance in *[Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus)*. They are the only ammunition used by the Kalash, [Bulldog](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bulldog), and [Gatling](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Gatling) Rifle (Bastard now uses [.44 Magnum](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/.44_Magnum) instead). The game no longer distinguishes between military-grade and dirty rounds, it is possible that the ammunition encountered throughout the game is a mixture of both, as many surface locations might still have access to military warehouses and other places with pre-war weapons and ammunition. Artyom can also craft 5.45mm ammo at the [workbench](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/workbench). Crafting 30 rounds costs 30 mechanical and 10 chemical materials. By default, it is possible to carry only 90 5.45 rounds, with the ammo pouches [upgrade](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Armour%23Upgrades) however, this amount can be increased to 180, but still remains considerably lower than in previous games. This means that Artyom now has to be more careful when using this type of ammo and might instead consider avoiding the fight. It is also a good idea to use weapons with lower fire rate but higher damage output (such as the Bulldog with upgraded long barrel) to maximize the impact each fired round has.

## Trivia
- 5.45x39mm is the only rifle-type round (discounting 7.62x54mm and 12.7x108mm) found in bulk in the Metro, and 7.62x39mm (the round that the AK-47 and AKM fire) is nonexistent, even though massive stockpiles of such rounds and weapons firing them should exist and said round would prove highly useful against mutants.
    - This may be due to the more common use of 5.45mm ammo, as most 7.62 weapons were phased out of service with the introduction of the AK-74 and further cemented by the introduction of the Kalash 2012 in the game universe.
- It is possible that the term "dirty" for the dirty rounds refers to both the condition and the homemade powder used; the casing is reused or homemade, which may cause fouling in the gun.
    - Using the dirty round only reduces the gun's firepower, not jamming the gun.
    - It's also possible that the primers are corrosive.
