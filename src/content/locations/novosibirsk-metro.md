**Novosibirsk Metro** (Russian: Новосибирский метрополитен) is a rapid transit system that runs underneath [Russia](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Russia)'s third largest city, [Novosibirsk](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Novosibirsk), and the only Russian metro system located eastward of the Ural mountains. It is a key location in the final part of *[Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus)* and its first DLC, [The Two Colonels](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Two_Colonels).

## Overview

Novosibirsk Metro has two lines and 13 stations in total: the [Dzerzhinskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dzerzhinskaya_Line) and [Leninskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Leninskaya_Line) lines. Before the [Great War](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/World_War_III), it was the third busiest metro system in Russia with daily ridership of over 200,000 people. One prominent feature is its covered bridge over the river Ob.

Most of the metro tunnels in Novosibirsk are fairly shallow, especially compared to metro systems in [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow) and St. Petersburg. This meant its level of radiation protection was not nearly as high, which plays an important role during the events of Metro Exodus and ultimately leads to the demise of its community of survivors.

While the [Moscow Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_Metro) was divided into several factions maintaining a harsh balance of power, the Novosibirsk Metro was unified under the military governance of [OSKOM](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/OSKOM). However, as anti-radiation medication supplies dwindled and food shortages worsened, civil unrest grew. Riots broke out, and the resulting civil war combined with [worm](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Worm) infestations ultimately destroyed the Novosibirsk Metro community.

## Metro Exodus

In the main game, [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) and [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller) descend into the Novosibirsk Metro during [The Dead City](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Dead_City_(Metro_Exodus_Level)) chapter, searching for information about a radiation-free zone that might hold a cure for [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Character))'s illness. The metro is filled with the remains of its former inhabitants and overrun by mutants, particularly [blind ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Blind_One) and worms.

In *The Two Colonels* DLC, the player explores the metro's final days through Colonel [Khlebnikov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Khlebnikov)'s perspective, witnessing the breakdown of order, the spread of worms through the tunnels, and the desperate last stand of OSKOM forces. The young boy [Kirill](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kirill_(Exodus)) is the sole known survivor.

## Trivia

- The real-world Novosibirsk Metro is indeed the only metro system east of the Urals in Russia and has two lines.
- The Novosibirsk Metro serves as a tragic counterpart to the Moscow Metro -- whereas Moscow's metro was deep enough to provide radiation protection, Novosibirsk's shallow tunnels doomed its inhabitants.
- The covered bridge over the Ob River is a distinctive real-world feature of the Novosibirsk Metro that also appears in the game.
