# Dry Station (Location)

Dry Station (Russian: Сухаревская, Sukharevskaya) is a heavily defended [bandit](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Faction)) station appearing in the video game [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Videogame)). Its real-world counterpart and progenitor is Sukharevskaya.

## Overview

Dry Station is a metro station in the post-apocalyptic Moscow wasteland that has been fortified into a bandit base. It is seen in the level "[Dry](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dry_(Level))", and mentioned in "[Dead City](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dead_City_1_(Level))", "[Market](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Market_(Level))", and "[Riga](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Riga_(Level))". [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) and [Bourbon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bourbon) make their way through the tunnels leading into Dry.

The layout of the station is of a typical modern day Moscow station; a long middle passage, with many offshoots leading towards the platforms. About halfway through the station is a wood and scrap metal barricade separating the station in two. On the one half is primarily the recreational area along with sleeping quarters, toilets (which a man can be seen running into if the player is stealthy), a rat-race (used for gambling, shooting targets), and target ranges with some arrows on it.

## In the novel Metro 2033

Dry station occupies the place where Sukharevskaya station is in the novel. The station in the novel holds very few similarities with this place. It is described as a very dark station by Artyom as light sources are scarce, and people can be seen gathering around various bonfires. People in the station who have higher ranking tend to have brighter bonfires. This is where [Khan](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Khan_(Character)) meets Artyom shortly before their arrival to the station, and then escorts him and [Ace](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ace) to the next station.

## Trivia

- In the English version, this station and the level that is played there are called 'Dry', possibly due to the fact that 'sukh' is the Slavic word root meaning 'dry' (Polish: suchy, Russian: сух), but the feminine version of 'dry' is 'sukhaya' (in Russian, like in the other Slavonic tongues, 'station' is a feminine noun). The station was named after the Sukharev's Towers erected nearby that was in turn named after Lavrientiy Sukharev, a 17th century military commander. Thus, proper translation of the name would be 'Sukharev Station'.
