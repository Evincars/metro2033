# Sevastopolskaya Station

Sevastopolskaya Station (Russian: Севастопольская) is a location in the [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034) novel that is also briefly mentioned in [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)). It is a key location of the [Sevastopolskaya HPP](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sevastopolskaya_HPP).

## Overview

The station is considered by its inhabitants a small Sparta, as every inhabitant from the youngest to the oldest is trained to fight. The reason is simple: the engineers of the station produce more than two thirds of the electricity of the Metro, through underground hydroelectric plants. Unfortunately, most of the generators are located south of the hermetic door, which means it must remain open, and so hordes of mutants attack Sevastopolskaya Station regularly.

In [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)), [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) hears one of the stories that the Sevastopolskaya citizens tell in the rest of the Metro, to deter attacks from the north. Also in the [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Video_Game)) game, Artyom hears one such tale during his brief stay in [Riga](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Riga_(Metro_2033_Level)) station, which is located behind a locked door after a left turn from the child offering to lead you to Bourbon.

## Contrast between Metro 2033 and Metro 2034

As can be read in Metro 2033, beyond Tulskaya station the line is abandoned and nobody knows what can happen there. Even [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza) won't colonize the line. It's a dead zone, the stations are empty and their installations damaged. There are no animals or monsters, not even rats.

A lone wanderer from Serpukhovskaya station (nobody knows why) once tried to venture from Tulskaya station to the end of the dead line. He described that the tunnels were very clean, without any sign of life and totally silent. He passed through many abandoned stations, in total darkness. And in every moment with a deep feeling of danger. Finally, the wanderer sees an eerie [ghost](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ghost) train at Sevastopolskaya station. Frightened, he decides to go back towards Tulskaya.

Later, in [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034), Sevastopolskaya is fully inhabited, leading to the assumption that earlier tales of the line were false rumours spread by the Sevastopolskaya citizens to deter attacks from the rest of the metro, or from Hanza.
