**Novosibirsk** (Russian: Новосибирск) is a [location](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Locations) in *[Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus)*. It serves as the main setting of the "[Dead City](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Dead_City_(Metro_Exodus_Level))" chapter, as well as [The Two Colonels](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Two_Colonels) DLC. Prior to its appearance in the game, some stories from the [Universe of Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Universe_of_Metro_2033_(Book_Series)) book series used Novosibirsk as a setting.

## Overview

The City of Novosibirsk was the third largest city of [Russia](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Russia) before the [Great War](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/World_War_III). During the war, Novosibirsk was the target of an extremely potent cobalt bomb. As a result, despite most of the buildings remaining intact, the radiation is more than eight times that of [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow). Despite the [Novosibirsk Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Novosibirsk_Metro) not being as deep as the one in Moscow, numerous people found shelter in it and survived for quite some time.

The shallow depth of Novosibirsk's metro system provided inadequate protection from surface radiation that would seep into the tunnels, requiring its inhabitants to regularly take [anti-radiation medications](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anti-rad). These began to run low, and the metro's hydroponic-based food supply could not feed its entire population. Riots soon broke out against the military leadership of [OSKOM](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/OSKOM), eventually resulting in a civil war that wiped out most of the population.

## Metro Exodus

In the Dead City chapter, [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) and [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller) travel to the ruins of Novosibirsk searching for a map to the radiation-free bunker facility thought to contain a cure for [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Character))'s radiation sickness. The surface of Novosibirsk is one of the most dangerous environments in the game, with extreme radiation levels, [blind ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Blind_One), and other deadly [mutants](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Mutants).

The story of Novosibirsk's fall is further explored in *The Two Colonels* DLC, where players experience the final days of the Novosibirsk Metro through the eyes of Colonel [Khlebnikov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Khlebnikov), witnessing the collapse of OSKOM's rule as supplies ran out, riots escalated, and [worms](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Worm) overran the tunnels.

## Trivia

- Novosibirsk was hit by a cobalt bomb rather than a conventional nuclear warhead, which is why the buildings remain standing but radiation levels are extremely high.
- The real-world Novosibirsk is indeed Russia's third largest city, located in Siberia on the Ob River.
- Novosibirsk's devastation serves as the emotional climax of Metro Exodus, as both Artyom and Miller must brave lethal radiation to save Anna.
