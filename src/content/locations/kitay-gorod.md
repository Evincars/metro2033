# Kitay-gorod

Kitay-gorod (Russian: Китай-город) is a transfer station that is visited by [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom), along with [Khan](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Khan_(Character)) and [Ace](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ace), in the novel [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)). It is divided into two territories controlled by two gangs.

## Overview

Khan, Ace, and Artyom reach Kitay-gorod from [Sukharevskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sukharevskaya). Upon arriving at the station, they find out that two [gangs](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Faction)) reside there. The first gang is the Russian Bratva called the **Brother Slavs**; the second gang is Muslim Caucasians consisting of Azerbaijanis and Chechens. These gangs come from the Kaluzhsko-Rizhskaya line and are usually called Kaluzkhys or Rizhskys by the citizens of the [Moscow Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_Metro).

The characters become separated during an attack from [Tretyakovskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Venice_(Location)) and Artyom is forced to flee from the station.

## Trivia

- There are no laws or law enforcement at the station of Kitay-gorod; only anarchy and crime reign at the station.
- It is in touch and connected with Sukharevskaya, where caravaneers and travelers come and stay before leaving.
- The station is colloquially referred to as "Chinatown" in [Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Last_Light), as shown by the map in the D6 command room where you meet Anna at the start of the game. However, the actual name "Kitay-gorod" refers to the historical part of the city above it and its etymology is a little more complicated than that.
