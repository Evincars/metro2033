**Vladivostok** (Russian: Владивосток) is a [location](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Locations) of the [Universe of Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Universe_of_Metro_2033_(Book_Series)) that is also featured in [Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus), where it serves as the main setting of the [Sam's Story](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sam%27s_Story) [DLC](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Downloadable_Content).

## Overview

Vladivostok is a city in the Russian Far East located on the Sea of Japan, and is the country's largest Pacific port. Unlike most major cities in Russia, Vladivostok did not suffer a direct nuclear strike during the [Great War of 2013](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/World_War_III). Instead, the warheads were aimed in a straight line offshore, resulting in a giant tsunami upon detonation, devastating the city and killing most of the population. The survivors banded together under the leadership of [Captain Eduard Baranov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Captain_(Vladivostok)), who used the nuclear reactors from the submarine he commanded to power the city. His position was soon usurped by [Tom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Tom), an American arms dealer who brought the city under his harsh, but mostly fair rule with the assistance of [Klim](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Klim), his much more brutal second in command and former head of security for Tom's company.

Aside from its bandit problem, the city is also infested with mutants, particularly [humanimals](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Humanimal), but also [lurkers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Lurker), [watchmen](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Watchman), [shrimp](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Shrimp), and [worms](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Worm). The most dangerous of them, however, is the batwing, also known as Seraph, a particularly large and vicious [demon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Demon) that controls a swarm of smaller mutated [bats](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bat) and prowls the skies of the city looking for prey. Conversely, humanimals have become a crucial part of the city's economy, as the beasts are strong and trained relatively easily. Aside from fishing, humanimal trapping is the most common profession in the city.

## Metro Exodus

[Sam](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sam), an American ranger with the [Spartan Order](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spartan_Rangers), enters Vladivostok in order to return to his homeland in the Sam's Story DLC. While in Vladivostok Sam assists Tom in reaching a deal with Eduard Baranov to restore the submarine to an operational state in exchange for Tom eliminating Klim and returning control of the main settlement at the docks to Eduard. However, after Sam and Eduard returned to the docks with the fuel rods for the submarine, Klim attacked the docks with a large force of bandits loyal to him, having known his death was part of Tom's deal with Eduard.

This battle decimated the settlement at the docks, as Klim's bandits, on his orders, began killing anyone who resisted them or looked at them the wrong way. This included largely irreplaceable people such as the engineers responsible for creating and maintaining the docks power plant and trade in refurbished generators. Sam made it through the docks and killed Klim, but the settlement was already substantially damaged. Soon, Sam was faced with the decision to destroy the submarine as Eduard wanted to, or side with Tom for a chance to return to the United States, but it's unclear if Vladivostok ever recovered with its major leaders all either dead or having abandoned the settlement.

## Trivia

- The Captain mentions that the captured humanimals are sold to traders from the South, who trade weapons, ammo, and rice. Given Vladivostok's geographical location, this would imply that there are survivors in China and/or the Korean peninsula.
