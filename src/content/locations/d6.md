# D6

> "You know, I imagined everything just as it is. There's something about these objects... they have Soviet soul. But, you wouldn't know anything about that..."
> — Miller

D6 (sometimes written as D-6, Russian: Д-6), also referred to as Metro 2, is a desolate top secret government/military facility, featured towards the later part of [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Video_Game)), and prominently featured in [Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro:_Last_Light). A focal point of the game's plot, it's in this sealed facility that [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) must aid his companions in finding and arming the missile launching facilities outside [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow), necessary to strike at the [Dark Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones).

A location very similar to D6 is mentioned in [Metro 2035](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2035), but the name itself is never used and the place is only referred to as a "bunker". It's an important strategic point that saw a massive attack from the [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line), just like the [Battle for D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_for_D6), sometime between the events of the book and [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034).

## Overview

Located deep underground near the Barrikadnaya and Mayakovskaya Stations and west of [Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich), D6 is a massive bunker-like facility whose access routes are well-hidden in the [Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Apocalyptic_Metro_System), and whose existence is regarded as something of a legend among the underground dwellers. Information found at the [Lenin Library](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Archives_(Level)) allows Artyom, [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller), and a group of [Rangers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Rangers), each with their own specialty, to reach one of such entrances, located near [Kievskaya Station](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya).

The accesses consist of massive airlocks concealing rooms used for storing machinery and spare equipment, gradually leading further underground, to what appears to be the remains of an old mine shaft. Even deeper are the only known ways into the facility itself: automated monorails which lead directly to D6 via tunnels separated from the metro. This network is known as Metro-2, and unlike the main Metro is built on a monorail system instead of the standard two-rail design. Being built deeper underground, it is far more intact and traversable. There are at least six distinct lines, and it is possible to directly reach Telesentr (the Ostankino TV tower) and the Botanical Gardens in the more northern part of the city from D6, which is located in the western portion somewhere under the Green Line.

Of all the Metro-2 lines (designated C1–C6), Line C5 heads eastwards, Line C3 heads north/northeast, and C4 heads southwards towards Kievskaya. It's likely the Metro-2 trains connect to the major government installations: FSB headquarters, Vnukovo Airport, the Kremlin, and other important surface facilities.

## Layout

D6 itself is an enormous installation centered around a very large central shaft comprised of two main sectors and one smaller sector, each with four sub-sections: A1–A4, B1–B4, and D2. The perimeter of the shaft houses the Metro-2 automated monorails and the control station that directs electricity through the facility and controls the air re-circulation system. To descend into the facility proper, two rail carts can be utilized, spiraling downwards to the four main floors of the shaft:

- **Sector A** is likely the Command and Control level, with a Command Center located in Subsector A1 and a larger one in A4. It's in A1 where Artyom, Vladimir, Ulman, and Miller head to activate the missiles to strike at the Dark Ones. Both command facilities are fairly identical, with rows of computer banks and telephones connected to systems that allow for command and control of outside military forces. Lighted maps of Moscow display the status of launching facilities. In Metro: Last Light, the Command Center in A4 is the main one, and is where Artyom receives his mission to kill the remaining Dark One in the Botanical Gardens. Direct elevator access for two of the Metro-2 monorails is available here, via elevators C3 and C4.
- **Sector B** contains domiciles, storage, and laboratories, where the base's staff would reside and conduct research. It's here that Artyom and the Rangers take up residence, in Subsector B2. The area is well equipped, with a kitchen, lounge areas, gymnasium, firing range, and ample quarters. Sector B4, as revealed in Metro: Last Light, is the place in which the Dark Ones hibernate while their brethren were wiped out on the surface.
- **Sector D**, the deepest part of the facility, lies beneath the main shaft and can be accessed by an elevator marked D2. This is where the nuclear reactor and main generators lie, along with the control facilities necessary to maintain them. Elevator access directly to the monorail level is available from the reactor in case of elevator failure.

On every level of the facility are electric trams used for the transport of personnel and materials, though most are inoperable after 20 years due to power failure. Massive sealed doors on all the floors of D6 apparently lead to unknown parts of the facility, including huge storage rooms filled with military hardware — tanks and other armored vehicles, missile launchers (including what appear to be RT-2UTTKh "Topol-M" Mobile Intercontinental Ballistic Missile units), huge stashes of ammunition and weapons, and so on. The facility has remote control of many other missile launchers outside D6, including the BM-30 Smerch mobile rocket launcher, although most facilities are destroyed before the Rangers' arrival, with only one unit on the outskirts of Southeastern Moscow being active.

Beyond D6, the larger Metro 2 facility is divided up into numbered sectors:

- **Sector 1** — Also referred to as Sector D6. Contains the D6 bunker complex.
- **Sector 5** — Station located on the C-4 Line. Connects to a pumping station and drainage pool, which ultimately connects to the larger metro system near Kievskaya.
- **Sector 23** — Connects to the ground floor of the Korolev Performance Hall.
- **Sector 42** — Station connects to a sewer system that opens up onto the surface near the Museum of Cosmonautics.

## Appearances

### Metro 2033

When Artyom and his group reach D6, the facility is sealed off and in a state of complete abandonment; its main facility is filled with toxic gases, requiring the use of a [gas mask](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Gas_mask), and it completely lacks electricity. After a brief struggle with the main control station, [Vladimir](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Vladimir) manages to reactivate the barest minimum to allow Artyom to manually reactivate the air filters and pump out the toxic air, which allows a restart of the main electric systems. However, the back-up generators run dry shortly afterwards. Because of this, Artyom and Miller are forced to travel into the deepest bowels of the facility, which are flooded and infested by strange [amoeba-like creatures](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Giant_Amoeba), with a very huge [specimen](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Biomass_(Mutant)) hindering access to the reactor. Artyom is forced to reactivate the nuclear reactor manually, during which the massive growth attacks him with a tentacle-like whip. After successfully re-activating it, the creature appears exhausted and dying. While Artyom and Miller head to the tower, the rest of the Ranger group stays in D6, preparing to launch the BM-30 missile strike against the Dark Ones.

In the novel, [Melnik](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Melnik), a group of commando [Stalkers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Stalkers), Artyom, and [Anton](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anton) go to D6 from the station [Park Pobedy](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Park_Pobedy). Melnik, Anton and the group of stalkers continue towards the rocket base, while Artyom and [Ulman](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ulman) exit D6 through the Mayakovskaya station, to continue to the surface, to the Ostankino tower.

### Metro: Last Light

D6 plays a pivotal role in *Metro: Last Light*. One year after the destruction of the Dark Ones, the Rangers have taken and fortified the military complex. It is the first location in which Artyom starts his mission and serves as his second home after [Exhibition](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Exhibition_(Location)). In this time, D6 became revered in the metro as a place of wonder — where advanced technological miracles would soon be unearthed that would save the people of the metro. Inside, however, appeared to only be more weapons to wage war with, and eventually an enemy thought dead. Unknown to the Rangers, a Red Army [operative](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Lesnitsky) managed to sneak away with several [biological weapons](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Virus) that [General Korbut](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Korbut_(Character)) planned to use to bloodlessly conquer the whole metro.

Despite all attempts to stop the Red Line and reveal the Communist plot, the Rangers fail to prevent Korbut's plans to steal all the samples of the virus from D6. A massive battle for D6, and the climax of Metro: Last Light, has the Rangers of the Order and the Red Army warring on the platform, culminating in the loss of the Rangers. Battered and unable to fight the sheer number of enemies, Miller and Artyom execute a fail-safe contingency that will destroy D6 (with everyone in it) in order to save the metro from Korbut. The plan succeeds or fails depending on the player's [actions](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moral_Point) throughout the game. In the [C'est La Vie](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/C%27est_la_Vie_(Achievement)) [ending](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Endings), D6 is destroyed, with all the Rangers, including Artyom, deemed martyrs. In the [Redemption](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Redemption_(Achievement)) ending, D6 and the Rangers are saved, along with the entire metro.

### Metro Exodus

D6 appears briefly in the beginning of the game, as Artyom ventures to and from it, searching for an outside radio signal. After escaping the Metro and finding the "Ark" facility, Miller mentions that it's built exactly like D6, as both facilities were constructed using the same standard design. This allowed the Rangers to quickly regroup after being captured, and swiftly search the facility to find Anna.

## Trivia

- Given the desolate state of the facility, it could be speculated that it was abandoned and sealed soon after the [Great War of 2013](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Great_War_of_2013), as D6 was built to house conventional weapons and combat vehicles, as well as a storage place for experimental biological weapons. Thus, it likely only had enough rations for its personnel over a comparatively short time.
- Both "D-6" and "Д-6" variants of the name are used in the game. It's justified as the cursive form of the capital Д letter in the Cyrillic alphabet looks like the Latin D.
- Inside the D6 control room, many *Metro 2033* novels can be found.
- The creatures infesting D6 in Metro 2033 are no longer seen in Metro: Last Light. It's possible that they were wiped out by the Rangers.
- Due to the large number of vehicles and weapons, it's possible that the Russians were preparing for a conventional invasion instead of a nuclear war.

## In Real Life

D6, also known as Metro-2, is an underground transport system used by the Russian government as a shelter. This system connects critical institutions of the government and military departments. Construction of D6 was started in the 1960s. Tunnels of this system can be used by trains and cars.
