# Metro 2033

> "Dear Muscovites and guests to our capital! The Moscow metro is a form of transportation which involves a heightened level of danger."
> — Opening novel quote

**Metro 2033** (Russian: Метро 2033), by Russian journalist and author [Dmitry Glukhovsky](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dmitry_Glukhovsky), tells the story of a young man named [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) who traverses the dangers of his [apocalyptic world](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_Metro) to save it from [a new threat from the north](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones). The book describes the consequences of an atomic war: its only survivors strive for existence in the mazes of the Moscow metro system some two decades after a [nuclear apocalypse](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/World_War_III). The novel contains elements of science fiction, post-apocalyptic fiction, and dystopia, in which Russia's present-day society is meticulously analysed and described.

In 2009, [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034) was published. Although set in the same world, it isn't a direct sequel to Metro 2033 and does not feature the same protagonist. Glukhovsky later wrote another book — [Metro 2035](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2035) — which is set two years after the first novel and continues the story of Artyom.

## Plot Synopsis

One day returning from his duty as the tunnel guard, [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) meets a stranger calling himself [Hunter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Ranger)). He is revealed to be a friend of Artyom's stepfather [Sukhoi](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Alex), and upon hearing about the dangers the station is facing, tasks Artyom to go to Polis and find a man named Melnik, delivering a message, while Hunter himself goes to deal with the problem firsthand.

After escorting a caravan, Artyom meets a man named [Bourbon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bourbon). Their travels come to a tragic end when Bourbon seemingly slips into insanity and dies. Luckily, a mysterious stranger arrives — [Khan](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Khan_(Character)) — who helps Artyom plan a route to his ultimate goal, [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis).

After making their way through a perilous tunnel, Khan and Artyom reach [Kitai Gorod](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kitay-gorod), where they become separated during an attack by the [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich). While fleeing, Artyom meets an [old man](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Mikhail_Porfirevich) who helps him reach [Kuznetsky Most](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kuznetsky_Most). Captured and nearly executed, Artyom is rescued by the [Revolutionaries](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Revolutionaries) and eventually dropped off at [Paveletskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Paveletskaya).

After escaping a sentence of latrine-cleaning, Artyom wanders into the tunnels and is offered shelter at [the Watchtower](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Watchtower), a monastery of sorts, before resuming his journey. He arrives at [Polyanka](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polyanka) and overhears a discussion about [Metro-2](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2), a mysterious government subway system, before finally reaching Polis and finding [Melnik](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Melnik).

At a council meeting, Artyom explains the situation at VDNKh. Although the council cannot spare aid, a sect of the Polis Council offers to help by sending Artyom to the [Great Library](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_State_Library) on the [surface](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Surface) to retrieve a rare book. Joining him are Melnik, a second stalker named Ten, and the young Brahmin Daniel. Daniel is killed by a [librarian](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Librarian), but Artyom returns with a map to a legendary location named [D-6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)).

Artyom and Melnik formulate a plan to help VDNKh by discovering the entrance to [D-6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)) and launching pre-war missiles on the lair of the Dark Ones. Their journey takes them through [Kievskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya), past the [Great Worm Cult](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Savage_Cannibals_of_the_Great_Worm_Cult) at Park Pobedy, and along the secret [Metro 2](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2) line beneath the Metro, where they encounter a [huge entity](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Biomass_(Mutant)) beneath the Kremlin.

After arriving at D-6, Artyom and a soldier named [Ulman](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ulman) travel to the surface to designate the lair of the Dark Ones for the missile strike, climbing the [Ostankino Tower](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ostankino_Tower_(Location)). In a final vision, Artyom confronts a Dark One who announces that Artyom is their chosen one, and he understands their true goal — peace, a desire to reunite with humanity. But it is too late: the missiles strike, and the Dark Ones are annihilated. Metro 2033 ends with Artyom, stricken with grief, removing his gas mask and silently leaving down the stairs of the tower, for home, for the Metro.

### Epilogue: The Gospel According to Artyom

In the expanded epilogue, Artyom describes his time after Ostankino Tower and the fate of the [Dark Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones). Once returning to [VDNKh](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Exhibition_(Location)) he is welcomed like a hero, yet he thinks of himself as a murderer. He recalls a childhood trip to the [botanical gardens](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Botanicheskiy_Sad) with [Zhenya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Zhenya) and [Vitalik the Splinter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Vitali), and a non-hostile encounter with a Dark One who left the words "You are the First" in his mind — an encounter that had inoculated him against the Dark Ones' messages. See also: [The Gospel According to Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Gospel_According_to_Artyom).

## History

The novel first appeared online in 2002, but was first being written when Glukhovsky was no older than 16. First titled *Underground*, this version consisted of only 13 of the story's eventual 20 chapters, and ended abruptly with Artyom's death from a stray bullet. Publishers rejected it, but once posted online it became an interactive experiment, drawing in thousands of readers from around Russia.

In 2005, it was reworked, expanded, and printed by an established publisher as *Metro 2033*, quickly becoming a nationwide bestseller. In 2007, Glukhovsky was awarded the Encouragement Award of the European Science Fiction Society in the Eurocon contest in Copenhagen. By 2009, over 400,000 copies had been sold in Russia alone, with foreign rights sold to more than 20 countries.

The FPS video game [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Videogame)) by [THQ](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/THQ) was released worldwide on the 16th of March, 2010 for PC and Xbox 360.

## Criticism

Despite astounding success, Metro 2033 was not without criticism. Some readers found it "aimless" or "empty" as a result of limited large-scale world building, while others felt aspects were described in too much detail. A common complaint concerns Artyom's tendency to stumble into danger and be saved by coincidence, placing pressure on the finale. English readers have also criticized the translation, noting difficult sentences and hard-to-read cover maps that make Artyom's journey difficult to follow.

## Trivia

- While quite original, Metro 2033 owes some of its finer genre points to the post-apocalyptic works that came before it, with subtle nods to [Fallout's](https://fallout.fandom.com/wiki/Portal:Fallout) [Master](https://fallout.fandom.com/wiki/Master) through the [creature of the Kremlin](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Biomass_(Mutant)) and to *Roadside Picnic*.
