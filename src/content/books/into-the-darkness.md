# Into the Darkness

***Into the Darkness*** (Russian: **Во мрак**) is a novel that is part of the [Universe of Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Universe_of_Metro_2033_(Book_Series)), a long-running series of short stories, novellas and novels written by different authors, all set in the world of [Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Series). *Into the Darkness*, written by Russian author Andrey Dyakov, was originally published in July of 2011. Although there is currently no English version of the book, *Into the Darkness* has been translated into a number of other European languages. The novel was translated into Polish by Paweł Podmiotko, whilst Tamás Kisbali worked on the Hungarian translation.

The book is a direct sequel to *[Towards the Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Towards_the_Light_(Novel))* and the second chapter of Andrey Dyakov's trilogy, followed by the third and last book *[Beyond the Horizon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Beyond_the_Horizon_(Novel))*. Just like its predecessor, *Into the Darkness* is set in [St. Petersburg](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/St._Petersburg). It has been promoted as a more mature, artful, and serious tale than the first novel.

## Synopsis
The novel features the same surviving protagonists from the previous story and takes place after the events of *Towards the Light*. As witnessed by the crew of the "Babel" offshore oil platform, inhabitants of the [Moshchny Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshchny_Island) perish in the fires of a nuclear explosion. Questions arise as to who and for what reasons would strike the peaceful society of the island, annihilating any hope of a brighter future for the few survivors of the [Catastrophe](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/World_War_III).

Dwellers of the [St. Petersburg Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/St._Petersburg_Metro) are accused of committing this atrocity. The "Babel" oil rig crew send them an ultimatum: the individuals responsible for the bombing are to be found within a week, otherwise all tenants of the subway will face a fate similarly deadly to that of the Moshchny citizens. The investigation becomes Taran's responsibility, but the situation becomes even more complicated by the sudden disappearance of Gleb, his foster-son. Questions multiply, but the path to the truth is a difficult one for it lies in darkness - at the very bottom of the human soul.
