# Metro 2034

**Metro 2034** (Russian: Метро 2034) is the original sequel to the [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)) novel. Although it is set in [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow), just one year after the events of the first book, it is not a direct sequel, since it follows the adventures of a mostly different set of characters. Widely popular, it sold some 300,000 copies in just six months in 2009, which made it [Russia](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Russia)'s biggest local bestseller at the time.

The book was also published online for free via the Metro 2034 Official Web Site, through which over a million visitors have read it. Glukhovsky turned the book into a multimedia art project, inviting the Russian electronic performer Dolphin to compose an original soundtrack for the novel, while artist Anton Gretchko created its oil-painted image gallery.

## Synopsis

Unlike its predecessor, Metro 2034 trades a sprawling cross-Metro quest for a smaller, more intimate and literary story centred on the southern reaches of the Metro. It follows a mostly new cast: the aging [Hunter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Ranger)), the old chronicler [Homer](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Homer) who dreams of writing a book that will outlast the darkness, and [Sasha](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sasha_(Metro_2034)), a young woman living in exile with her father. When the besieged station of [Sevastopolskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sevastopolskaya) is cut off from the rest of the Metro, the three are drawn together on a journey through the tunnels that becomes a meditation on memory, mortality, and what it means to remain human underground.

## Translations

Metro 2034 has been translated into more than fifteen languages, with the English edition published by Gollancz in 2014.
