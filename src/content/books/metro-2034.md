# Metro 2034

***Metro 2034*** (Russian: **Метро 2034**) is the original sequel to the *[Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel))* novel. Although it is set in [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow), just one year after the events of the first book, it is not a direct sequel, since it follows the adventures of a mostly different set of characters. Widely popular, it sold some 300,000 copies in just six months in 2009, which made it [Russia](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Russia)'s biggest local bestseller at the time.

The book has also been published online for free via the [Metro 2034 Official Web Site](http://www.m2034.ru/), through which over a million visitors have read it. Glukhovsky turned the book into a multimedia art project; inviting the famous Russian electronic performer Dolphin to compose an original soundtrack for the novel, while artist Anton Gretchko created its oil-painted images gallery.

## Synopsis
It's the year 2034. From the events that started and ended in [VDNKh Station](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Exhibition_(Location)), not even a year has passed. The [Dark Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones) once considered as a deadly threat are gone for good, killed by [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) and his allies.

On the other side of the Metro, the inhabitants of [Sevastopolskaya Station](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sevastopolskaya_Station) are fighting for survival against new threats that constantly invade them. The fate of the station depends on weapon supplies, which suddenly cut short, along with missing caravans and communication.

To solve the mystery and to bring back the stability of supplies, a small group is sent: young [Ahmed](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ahmed), old unfulfilled chronicler [Homer](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Homer) and [Hunter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Ranger)) - once lost among the Dark Ones, now found but with a rather uncertain identity... Their small group is extended with [Sasha](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sasha_(Metro_2034)), the daughter of a banished station-master.

Who really is Hunter? Does he return the affection that Sasha feels for him? What secrets are hidden in the darkness of the Metro tunnels? And do the unlikely heroes manage to save the remaining humans who survived the catastrophe?
