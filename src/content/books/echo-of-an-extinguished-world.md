# Echo of an Extinguished World

***Echo of an Extinguished World*** (Polish: **Echo zgasłego świata**) is a book that is part of the [Universe of Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Universe_of_Metro_2033_(Book_Series)), a long-running series of short stories, novellas and novels written by different authors, all set in the world of [Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Series). *Echo of an Extinguished World*, written by numerous Polish authors, was originally published in March of 2016. Although there is currently no English version of the book, *Echo of an Extinguished World* has been translated into one other Slavic language - Russian. The book was translated to Russian by Leonid Dobkach and published in [Russia](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Russia) on the 18th of May, 2016.

It was the sixth entry in the series created by Poles. Similarly to its predecessors *[In the Firelight](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/In_the_Firelight_(Novel))* and *[Whispers of the Fallen](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Whispers_of_the_Fallen_(Novel))*, *Echo of an Extinguished World* is a collection of 14 short stories by 14 writers, which were selected for publication by a joint jury of admins and fans on the official Polish portal of the series: [metro2033.pl](http://metro2033.pl/). In [Poland](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Poland), it is available in both print and e-book formats. In Russia, it was released as an e-book file available for free download.

## Synopsis
Two of the stories contained within this collection are set in [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow): one reveals the secrets of a peculiar ceremony held at one of the stations, while the other recounts the crooked fate of a gang of common thieves caught by [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza) during a failed heist. Two other chapters of *Echo of an Extinguished World* expand the known [Universe of Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Universe_of_Metro_2033_(Book_Series)) by adding two entirely new locations to its repertoire - the [Stockholm Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stockholm_metro) in Sweden (where an outbreak of a hallucinogenic virus leads to the downfall of the [Solna centrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solna_centrum_metro_station) station) and [Dunmore Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunmore_Cave) in Ireland (where a mentally unstable Polish immigrant and a group of Irish people have lived through the war, but the arrival of a mutated, hyperintelligent spider-like being rives their community).

The rest of the book is set in [Poland](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Poland). One of the stories was written by Paweł Majka, author of *[The Promised District](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Promised_District_(Novel))*, and takes place in [Kraków's](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Krak%C3%B3w) Nowa Huta district during winter, featuring one of the main characters from Majka's novel. Several others take us to the Silesian region of the country, showing us how people have survived in old mines as well as the grim tunnels of [Project Riese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Riese). Elsewhere in the southern part of Poland, a religious cult has endured within [Giewont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giewont), whereas [Zabrze's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabrze) former mine houses a society that preforms a "lottery" every time a child is born to dictate who will leave to die on the surface. Up north in [Gdańsk Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gda%C5%84sk_Bay) people live on a set of connected boats, while the inhabitants of [Gdynia's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gdynia) military shelters try to reach other survivors by radio from the city's abandoned torpedo station. In the east, where [Warsaw](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Warsaw) lies, the [Varsovian Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Warsaw_Metro) has provided refuge for some of the capital's denizens.

## Trivia
- The gask mask seen on the book's front cover is the MP-6 "Apollo", designed and produced in Poland - used by the Polish Armed Forces since 2012, phasing out the earlier MP-5.
