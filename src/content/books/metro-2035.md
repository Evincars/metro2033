# Metro 2035

> "It was like a birth. Moscow was like a woman who had already died, but the children in her petrifying womb were still alive. And they wanted to be born, and they were crying there inside."
> — Dmitry Glukhovsky

**Metro 2035** (Russian: Метро 2035) is the sequel to [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)). Set after [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034), it's the official continuation of [Artyom's](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) story in the fictional universe of [Dmitry Glukhovsky's](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dmitry_Glukhovsky) Metro [franchise](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Series). An English version came out in early December of 2016.

## Overview

Metro 2035 is the sequel to both [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)) and [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034), whilst having its own unique tone and atmosphere. It has more sexual and political themes than [Glukhovsky's](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dmitry_Glukhovsky) previous works. It features returning characters such as [Sukhoi](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Alex), as well as new personas. Its story begins in a quite different Metro to that of 2033 and 2034, but by the end the world of post-apocalyptic Moscow is left turned almost completely on its head.

## Synopsis

By 2035, the strange world of [Glukhovsky's](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dmitry_Glukhovsky) post-apocalyptic [Moscow Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Apocalyptic_Metro_System) has changed considerably. The [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich) has adopted a more welcoming image, the [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) is struck by a mushroom blight and famine, and the [Rangers of the Order](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Rangers_of_the_Order) have grown suspiciously close with [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza) after heavy losses in a battle for control of a bunker.

[Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom), having willingly left the [Order](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Rangers_of_the_Order) and married [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Character)) in spite of [Miller's](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller) disapproval, is living a quiet life on [VDNKh](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Exhibition_(Location)). Yet he is convinced that on that fateful day at the [Ostankino Tower](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ostankino_Tower_(Location)), just before the Rangers rained fire on the [Dark Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones), he heard a transmission from outside of Moscow. He now wanders the surface almost daily to make radio contact with the outside world, ostracised as a madman and exposing himself to dangerous radiation.

His search is rewarded by the appearance of [Homer](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Homer), who wants to interview Artyom for a book and mentions a radio operator named Umbach who claims to have contacted survivors from [Polar Dawns](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polar_Dawns). Their journey takes them through Reich and Red Line stations, into slavery at Schiller (Pushkinskaya), and eventually to the surface complex at Balashikha — a field of radio jammers designed to keep Moscow hidden from the outside world.

Artyom discovers the terrible truth: the war between Russia and the West is still ongoing, and the last remnants of the Russian government — the [Invisible Watchers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Invisible_Watchers), led by Bessolov — secretly control all four major factions from a hidden bunker, keeping the Metro's people ignorant so they will never leave. Betrayed by nearly everyone, Artyom and [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Character)) load a car with supplies and drive east toward Vladivostok, some 9,000 kilometers distant, in search of a life beyond the tunnels.

## Metro: Last Light

Contrary to information given by [Glukhovsky](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dmitry_Glukhovsky) before publication, this book is not a direct continuation of [Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Last_Light) and is set a year after some of its events. The games and the books follow a different canon: Metro 2035 takes some inspiration from [4A Games](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/4A_Games) (such as the character of [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Character)) and her relationship with Artyom), yet omits many other aspects, making it more appropriate to call it a direct sequel to [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)), set one year after the events of [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034). The battle over a "bunker" sounds very similar to the [Battle for D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_for_D6), but no explicit mention of [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)) is ever made, and the little Dark One subplot is entirely absent.
