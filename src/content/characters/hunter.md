# Hunter

Hunter (Russian: Хантер, literally "Hanter") is an experienced [Ranger](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Category:The_Rangers) and old friend of [Sukhoi](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Alex), possibly due to a pre-war friendship. A key part of the story, he entrusts [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) with a mission to save the entire [Moscow Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_Metro).

Years later, he emerges as a new Führer of the fascist organization called the [Novoreich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/NovoReich).

> "If it's hostile, you kill it!" — Hunter

## Overview

As a ranger, Hunter is high-ranking and one of the best the [Order](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spartan_Rangers) has to offer, being trusted enough to traverse the [surface](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow) alone with heavy gear. Hunter lives by his codex of: "If it's hostile, you kill it". He vehemently supports trying to counter the threats that could destroy the entire metro system. When the [Dark Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones) begin to attack [Exhibition](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Exhibition_(Location)), he travels to the station. He seems to be well known around [VDNKh](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/VDNKh_Station_(Location)) and by [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom), whom he hands a postcard with the Statue of Liberty on it upon his arrival at the station.

His arrival sets into motion the storyline of the [novel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)) and the [video game](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Video_Game)). At the end of his [titular level](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Level)) he goes after the Dark Ones that attacked the station. He tells Artyom that if he is not back by morning, Artyom must take his ranger token to [Melnik](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller) in [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_(Location)) and report the current situation at Exhibition. He does not return, and thus, Artyom's journey begins. Everyone who knew him believed he dies. It would be better for the whole Metro if it were so...

## In the Games

### Artyom's Visions

Hunter is seen a couple of times in Artyom's visions as it can be understood that Artyom sees Hunter as his mentor.

He can be seen gunning down a Dark One that pushes him down, during the mission "[Chase](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Chase_(Metro_2033_Level))", after [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) collides with its [ghost](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ghosts_(Phenomenon)). This means Hunter likely killed it while entering/leaving Exhibition, and it is infinitely reliving its final moments. Being a ghost and still able to create telepathic visions for Artyom to see shows how powerful the dark ones telepathic abilities are, able to still be aware and project themselves in visions, even when dead and in limbo.

Near the end of the game, as Artyom is running through [a bizarre vision](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ethereal) while trapped by the dark ones, he hears Hunter's voice and runs towards him. As he reaches Hunter, he falls, when he turns over, a very bright light shines in Artyom's face, then Hunter emerges from the light and says: "If it's hostile, you kill it", he then throws a revolver to Artyom. Artyom uses the revolver to shoot the Dark One.

### Hunter's Field Base

There is a ranger hideout in a crumbling building on "[Dead City 1](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dead_City_1)" above a haberdashery shop, to the right of stairs leading to "[Dead City 2](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dead_City_2)", that apparently was frequented by Hunter and other rangers. This area is not suitable for staying for a long time as the room is exposed to the surface and need a gas mask to survive. There is a tape player on the table that has a recorded message from him. This tape was recorded approximately a few days before the [invasion of the dark ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Level)) while Hunter and possibly other Rangers, on account of his transmission, was heading to Exhibition in response to the frequent [Dark One](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones) attacks.

> "Red, I took the heavy gear with me and went north to Exhibition. They are having huge problems with our old friends. If I'm not back in a couple of days gather our men and tell the [Colonel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller). Good luck."

The message can be only played once. Activating the tape recorder again will play an instrumental song known as ['Market'](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_Original_Soundtrack).

### D6

In [Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Last_Light), it can be seen that Artyom transferred all his old postcards in his new room in D6. The Statue of Liberty postcard that Hunter gave to him is there too, alongside Hunter's ranger token from the first game.

### Exodus

In [Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus) Artyom is saved from taking a bullet by Hunter's ranger token, which he had been wearing.

### Metro 2039

He is set to return as main antagonist as he is now the new Furher of the fascist organization called NovoReich.

Hunter's fate is currently unclear in the continuity of the video games. He may have been killed, or he may have been taken by the Dark Ones as he is seen by Artyom during the Dark One's mind game in "[Ethereal](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ethereal)". However, this is only speculation. In [Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Last_Light), Khan mentions that Hunter is M.I.A. (probably because no body was found). It's likely that Hunter survived in the video game continuity as he is one of the protagonists of [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034).

## In the Book Series

### Metro 2033

Artyom first meets Hunter after returning from guard duty. He then sees Hunter again as he talks with [Sukhoi](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Alex) about the Metro's fate. Later that day Hunter asks Artyom to meet him near his tent and offers the young man an exchange of secrets. Artyom tells Hunter of his childhood expedition to the [Surface](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow) and its outcomes. Hunter seeks to close the barrier, and goes after the Dark Ones that attacked the station. After not returning, he is considered missing, and Artyom begins on his journey to Polis per Hunter's request.

### Metro 2034

> "I know that Hunter can be stopped. He has two sides... I've seen both. One wants blood, the other to protect people! (...) Simply the one inside, who kills people, is deceiving the other one. It tells him, that he has no other choice. One is tormented by hunger the other by longing... That's why Hunter is so eager to get to Tulska - both of his sides are! We have to separate one from another. We have to show him there is a choice..." — Sasha to Homer about Hunter's behavior

The encounter with the Dark Ones "broke" him by showing him his true colors: how savage and rotten he had always been as a human and how he used his position of a ranger to quench his thirst of blood. Unfortunately by showing him his "real reflection", the dark ones had awaken the evil within him, that didn't subdue but started to consume him. The man fled from them and avoided people, feeling remorse and fear of his new self. Hunter also started to hurt himself as a form of self-punishment for whom he had been and had become.

The ex-ranger appears one year later at [Sevastopolskaya Station](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sevastopolskaya_Station), where he took the role of brigadier - from that time he's mostly called that way by others.

Hunter is distinguished by his unnatural craving for violence and blood along with his badly scarred face. He barely talks and it is impossible to reason with him - once he sets his mind on the task he would do everything to complete it.

As Sevastopolskaya Station is set upon by an unknown danger, he declares himself to solve the station's problem along with [Homer](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Homer) and [Ahmed](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ahmed) whom he choose as his helpers.

Soon, Ahmed is killed by a monstrous mutant at Nagornaya Station. Not much later, Hunter and Homer arrive at [Tulskaya Station](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Tulskaya_Station) to learn that all caravans are stuck at it due to an unknown disease - a combination of plague and rabies. Hunter, having no other choice, decides to go to [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_(Location)) to get reinforcement troops and [flamethrowers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Flamethrower) to cleanse the whole station - again dragging Homer along.

After witnessing an inundation of Tulska Station, he settled down at Sevastopolskaya, where he, once more, took the role of brigadier.

It is worth noting that throughout the book [Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034_(Novel)), Hunter develops an interesting and unusual relationship with a much younger female called [Sasha](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sasha_(Metro_2034)). As the girl develops feelings for the cold ranger who has become a dark, broken shell of his former self, Hunter fails to return a sentiment of the same type. This continues throughout the story of the novel, with only a few exceptions of where Hunter shows more human emotions. Near the end of the book, during [Sasha](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sasha_(Metro_2034))'s presumed death, the former [ranger](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Rangers) finally reveals that he has begun feeling similar emotions of (unusual) 'love' towards his female companion, by shouting at his dying partner that he "needs" her. However, by then, it's too late and Sasha is just about to die.

### Metro 2035

Hunter is mentioned as being alive by the time of the events that take place in [Metro 2035](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2035), but he does not play any role in its story. Sasha, however, reveals that Homer greatly romanticized Hunter's personality in his book. In actuality, Hunter was a chronic alcoholic. Sasha describes him as carrying a flask in one hand and a Stechkin pistol in the other, and often being barely able to walk straight.

Hunter is depicted as a man with a bear-like frame. It's also noted that he has animal-like instincts, doesn't need much light, and has great hearing.

## Trivia

### In the Game

- How exactly he manages to carry his heavy gear to VDNKh across the surface is unknown, although he could just be either a very skilled or lucky individual. When Hunter arrives in VDNKh he is carrying a large duffel bag, possibly containing the heavy gear in question.
  - Supposedly, he forgets the bag at the entrance to VDNKh station even after he defended it.
- Judging by his ranger token, it's possible that Hunter was formerly a member of a special forces unit before the nuclear holocaust.
  - Writing on the Hunter's token means "SPARTA" and is written in Cyrillic lettering ("СПАРТА").
- He refers to [Nosalises](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nosalises) as "Tunnel Trash".
- Hunter appears during the [Ethereal](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ethereal) level without his helmet.
- The "red" in Hunter's recording is either a reference to Redrick "Red" Schuhart, a Stalker from Roadside Picnic or the Ranger [Krasnov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Krasnov) ("Red") manning the customs next to Miller's office in Polis.
- He is mentioned by Khan at the beginning of Metro: Last Light.
- Hunter is mentioned again by Anna at the beginning of the [Undercity](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Undercity_(Metro_Last_Light_Level)) level and once more in the Chronicles Pack level "[Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Chronicles_Pack_DLC_Level))", during the level's intro.
  - From her dialogue, it's clear that Anna admired and respected Hunter, who may have been her mentor.
- Many fans originally believed Hunter to be the mysterious prisoner on the Nazi Train mentioned during the original Metro: Last Light trailers. However, it was soon revealed to be the [Baby Dark One](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Baby_Dark_One).

### In the Book Series

#### Metro 2033

- In the novel, Hunter doesn't give Artyom his ranger token but a "metal capsule made from a machine gun cartridge" that was later revealed to contain a message written on paper.
- In the novel, Hunter gives Artyom a spent cartridge from an AK, originally given to him from Melnik. Melnik later gives similar cartridges to [Oleg](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Oleg) at [Kievskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya).

#### Metro 2034

- [Sasha](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sasha_(Metro_2034)) fell in love with Hunter, their unique relationship was a base for Homer's book.
- The ex-ranger believes that Homer is a key to his lost sanity as the man seems to remind him of a part of himself he lost, during his encounter with the Dark Ones.
- Hunter's confrontation with the Dark Ones had a profound effect on his mind. He developed a great deal of hatred for them and that is the reason why Hunter, after he slaughtered a black person on Avtozavodskaya station, is in fear of his appearance mistaking him for one of them.
- In the novel and the [board game](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Board_Game)), Hunter is depicted as being bald. However, in the game he has short hair (as seen in "[Ethereal](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ethereal)").
- In the novel, it's mentioned twice that Hunter wore a long (possibly ankle length) brown trench coat, but in the game he doesn't have one.
- It's also mentioned in the [novel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)) that he is armed with a Stechkin automatic pistol with a silencer, stock, and laser sight attached.
- He states that his name is not of interest, but Artyom can call him by his profession "The Hunter".
- According to Anna, Khan, Artyom, and Melnik, Hunter was the best of the Rangers.
