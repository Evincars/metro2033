# Polis

> "The last place on Earth where people live as people, where they still remember what the word human means, and how it should sound."
> — *Metro 2033* novel

**Polis** (Russian: *Полис*) is an independent collection of stations located at the heart of the [Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Apocalyptic_Metro_System), situated below the [Moscow State Library](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_State_Library) and the General Staff building of the Armed Forces of the Russian Federation. It's situated very close to the [Kremlin](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kremlin).

- **Base location:** Polis
- **Leader:** the Polis Council
- **Disposition towards the player:** Friendly
- **Allies:** [Rangers of the Order](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spartan_Rangers), [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza)
- **Enemies:** [Mutants](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Mutants), [Bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Faction))

## Overview

Polis is formed by the Arbatskaya, Aleksandrovskiy Sad, Biblioteka Lenina, and Borovitskaya stations. Above ground is the [Moscow State Library](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_State_Library), formally known as the Lenin Library. Throughout the Metro, Polis is known as a station with wealth and power. The platforms which encompass Polis seem to have been taken up by various machinery, with near building-like structures taking the place of housing.

In the novel, there is a caste system consisting of:

- **Shudra** — the servants
- **Vaishya** — a group of dedicated Polis merchants
- **[Kshatriya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_Kshatriya)** — composed of former [Russian Army](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Russian_Armed_Forces) soldiers, special forces, and government agents who make up the defense force
- **Brahmins** — the pre-war scientists, teachers, and other important keepers of knowledge

Every individual in Polis decides which caste they want to enter when they turn 18, and the four stations denominate and separate the four groups. The Brahmins are known to pay great amounts for [Stalkers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Stalkers) to go to the Library to collect books. In the game [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Videogame)), the hierarchy is not set into groups but instead into one: the Polis Council.

Polis is a unique city-state of the Metro. Only there do the keepers of old pre-war knowledge remain in substantial quantity. As other Metro states slowly descended into the abyss of chaos and ignorance, scientists from everywhere came to Polis. Only there could one meet elderly professors from pre-war universities — the last artists, artisans, musicians, poets, physicists, chemists, biologists, and mathematicians who kept the knowledge achieved by thousands of years of human history. The station is also considered to be the birthplace of the Rangers of the Order.

## Protection

In the novel, because Polis is seen by many as the last hope for humanity — the last bastion and the last think-tank — it has a sizable defensive force. It is widely believed by the inhabitants and other Metro occupants that a hidden rule holds: an attack on Polis means an attack on everyone.

In the game, Polis is protected by the [Kshatriya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_Kshatriya), an umbrella term for Polis' military branches, which includes a very large detachment of [Ranger](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spartan_Rangers) forces as well as ordinary town guards and militia. Protection is around the clock, using the most advanced equipment. Deployed at the entrance is a large concrete bunker reinforced with metal plates, featuring a small retracting pillbox, a large spherical turret closely resembling the AK-130 naval cannon, and quad-mounted [DShK machine guns](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/DShK_12.7mm_Machinegun). Similarly, in the novel Polis boasts a small but professional military corps made up of former FSB officers and military leadership, whose buildings were right above the stations of Polis when [the war](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Great_War_of_2013) broke out.

## The Library

Polis is situated right under the very center of the city that once stood above it. Nearby looms the gigantic Moscow State Library, the biggest information storage of the old world. After the war, it was called the "Great Library" and became a religious and sacred place for Polis dwellers; the rest of the Metro respectfully calls it "the Library". Only Polis of all the Metro powers can afford sending stalkers for books and other pre-war items considered "old world technology" — only there is knowledge still valued highly enough to risk one's life to acquire it. Because it was the last science center, Polis was never attacked, threatened, or devastated, but rather traded with and meticulously guarded; all Metro powers were ready to defend it. Even the war between the Hansa and the [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) did not touch Polis.

## Trivia

- When Artyom and Ulman arrive in Polis, they enter the Borovitskaya station. This location is part of an interchange point between three other stations comprising the whole city of Polis: Biblioteka Imeni Lenina, Arbatskaya, and Alexandrovsky Sad.
- The Greek "polis" (πόλις), or "city-state", was the term coined probably by one of the station dwellers, and it was only a matter of time before it became official. The name also fits the Rangers' Spartan theme.
- In both games, Polis itself is largely unexplored, and Artyom has little to do in those levels, as most are taken over by cutscenes. The councils in both games are dramatically different.
- When Artyom enters Polis in the novel, he is forced to wear sunglasses due to the bright lighting of the station.
