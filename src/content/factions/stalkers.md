# Stalkers

A **stalker** (Russian: Сталкер) is the general term in the [Moscow Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_Metro) — and parts of [Poland](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Poland) and elsewhere — for anyone who ventures onto the irradiated [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow) surface to recover valuable supplies, often risking life and limb to keep their communities alive.

## Overview

Stalkers search for [fuel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fuel), [ammunition](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ammunition), and other salvage to sell in the Metro or keep for themselves. Some keep small surface hideouts in ruined buildings, though most live in stations. They often work alone or in small teams but universally aid one another — leaving supply drops, marking swamp routes with brightly coloured guideposts, and signalling with [flashlights](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Flashlight). Many have pre-war military backgrounds and are well-armed, wearing armoured vests, hazmat suits, and long trench coats.

A stalker's life expectancy is very low, so those who venture out are seen as either thrill-seekers or suicidal — [Bourbon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bourbon) calls them "crazy devils." Yet everyone relies on them, giving them an almost godly status. Very few living stalkers are seen in the games; most are desiccated corpses [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) can loot, underscoring their deadly profession.

## Organization

While many stalkers work independently to support themselves or their families, some acknowledge the benefits of teaming up — for example the group in the [Developer Pack](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Developer_Pack_(DLC)) mission [Spider Lair](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spider_Lair). Political [factions](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Factions) also commission large stalker groups: [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_(Location)) employs the [Spartans](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spartans) to recover pre-war technology, and the [Nazis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich) who capture Artyom in "[Ashes](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ashes)" are stalkers. In [Novosibirsk](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Novosibirsk), [OSKOM](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/OSKOM) ran its own largely military stalkers, dedicated to finding satellite maps and the vital [Radioprotector](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Radioprotector).

## Novels

Stalkers have a more central role in the novels than in the games, effectively replacing the [rangers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Rangers). Admired for their skill and bravery, they are the faction [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller) belongs to in the book, leading a commando unit into [Park Pobedy](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Park_Pobedy) to rescue Artyom. They are described as armed with short assault rifles bearing laser sights and silencers, wearing heavy bullet-proof vests, massive titanium helmets, and large titanium shields with eye slits. In the Kraków-set novels *[The Promised District](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Promised_District_(Novel))* and *[The Promised Human](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Promised_Human_(Novel))*, independent stalkers are known as "ghosts" (Polish: *duchy*), respected for their survival skills and independence from the large factions.

## Trivia

- The term "stalker" derives not from the *S.T.A.L.K.E.R.* franchise but from the Strugatsky brothers' novel *Roadside Picnic*, where it first described those brave (or crazy) enough to enter the Visitation Zones.
- The stalkers' surface identification signal is three circles drawn with a flashlight; the response copies the same three circles.
- Most Metro dwellers view stalkers as heroes — "the knights in shining armour" — respected even more than former Metro workers.
- In the books, some stalkers were criminals sent to the surface as a form of capital punishment.
- In *[Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus)*, OSKOM's stalkers were better equipped than their Moscow counterparts but suffered far higher casualties; dozens were found dead by Colonel [Khlebnikov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Khlebnikov).
