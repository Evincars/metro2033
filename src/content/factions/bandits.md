# Bandits

**Bandits** are typical criminals of the [Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Apocalyptic_Metro_System) — loosely banded groups of cutthroats, robbers, drifters, and murderers. Hostile to the player, they appear across *Metro 2033*, *Metro: Last Light*, the Chronicles Pack DLC, and *Metro Exodus*.

## Background

Twenty years after humanity retreated underground, supplies are scarce: food, filtered [water](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Apocalyptic_Metro_System), and [weapons](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Weapons) are extremely precious, and many stations refuse outsiders. This drives some people toward the deadliest forms of anarchism — taking what they need to survive. Bandit groups are the result.

Gangs usually stay small for mobility, though some band together in hopes of building empires, occasionally seizing entire stations. They plunder, kill, torture, and enslave, and some wear hockey masks as a symbol. Groups vary widely in command structure, weapons, and size:

- The gang at [Dry Station](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dry_Station_(Location)) is comparatively well-armed with [Kalashes](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kalash_(AK-74M)) and [Shamblers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Uboinik_(Shambler)) but lives in scrap shacks.
- The gang in the *Last Light* level of the same name is larger and more territorial but poorly equipped, using carbine [Lolifes](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Lolife) and long [Ashots](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ashot).

A unifying trait is their speech, full of prison jargon (especially in the Russian dub), suggesting many were part of the penal system before the [Great War](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Great_War_of_2013).

## Appearances

### Metro 2033

[Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) first encounters bandits while traveling with [Bourbon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bourbon) toward [Market](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Market_Station_(Location)) station, passing traps, dead caravanners, and empty camps. After fighting through a bandit camp and crossing the surface, the pair reach Dry Station, where Bourbon is captured. Artyom escapes and reaches the holding area just as the bandit leader is about to execute Bourbon; Bourbon grabs the leader's [pistol](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Revolver) and both are seemingly killed, though the *Last Light* Chronicles Pack implies Bourbon survived. For the rest of the game only dead bandits are found, since the surrounding territory belongs to the [Communists](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Communist) and [Nazis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nazi).

### Metro: Last Light

A large group of bandits is fought in the level "[Bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Metro_Last_Light_Level))," where they ambush Red Army deserters and refugees, taking women and children captive. Artyom has [opportunities](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moral_Points#Bandits) to rescue captives. These bandits are lightly armored and easily dealt with using stealth.

#### Gangsters

In [Venice](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Venice_(Location)), a more organized version of bandits — **gangsters** — run the station rather than terrorizing it, robbing freely with little opposition. A leader named **Kisliy** helped the [Reds](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) deliver virus containers to [Oktyabrskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Oktyabrskaya). [Pavel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Pavel_(Chronicles_Pack_Level)) killed a gangster leader before later being captured by the same gangsters and fighting his way out of their base beneath Venice.

### Metro Exodus

Bandits are active across post-apocalyptic Russia, with a large presence in the [Volga](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Volga) region, where they raid caravans, harass locals, and clash with the [Church of the Water Tsar](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Church_of_the_Water_Tsar). They also appear in the [Valley](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Valley), where the [Children of the Forest](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Children_of_the_Forest) largely wipe them out.

#### Trappers

Drawn to rumors of prosperity, bandits swarmed [Vladivostok](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Vladivostok) until [Tom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Tom) and his group arrived. After the brutal methods of Tom's lieutenant [Klim](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Klim), surviving bandits stopped preying on civilians and now focus on capturing and taming local [humanimals](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Humanimal) to sell. They remain fiercely territorial.

### In the Novels

Bandits are never seen in the novels but are mentioned often. They periodically raided [VDNKh](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/VDNKh_Station_(Location)) from the southern tunnels, killing Artyom's friend Vitalik. [Kitay-gorod](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kitay-gorod) is run by two rival criminal gangs, similar to Venice. In *[Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034)*, characters wrongly theorize that [Tulskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Tulskaya) has been overrun by bandits.

## Trivia

- The hockey masks worn by some bandits resemble the one worn by Jason Voorhees from *Friday the 13th*.
- Bourbon is in debt to the bandits and may be a former bandit himself.
- Bandits around Venice often wear [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich) gear, traded to them by the Nazis for funds.
- They are the first hostile faction — and first hostile humans — Artyom faces in *Metro 2033*.
- Bandits can sometimes be heard humming "Gop-Stop."
