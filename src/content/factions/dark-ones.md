# Dark Ones

The **Dark Ones** (Russian: Чёрные, "black ones") are a sapient species descended from humans who survived the nuclear apocalypse and adapted to the new world. Unlike [Humanimals](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Humanimal), who are also derived from humans, the Dark Ones are far better suited to the heavy radiation of cities such as [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow) and [Novosibirsk](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Novosibirsk). They are also known as **Homo Novus** ("new man").

> "We want peace..."
> — The Dark Ones

## Overview

Though rarely seen, the Dark Ones serve as the driving antagonists of *Metro 2033*, appearing as a new mutant threat descending from the surface toward [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom)'s home station of [VDNKh](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Exhibition_(Location)) (Exhibition, in the game).

## Metro 2033

The Dark Ones originated in a hive on the surface at [Botanicheskiy Sad](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Botanicheskiy_Sad) (Botanical Gardens), north of VDNKh. They were first revealed to humanity after a young Artyom opened the great [Hermetic Door](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hermetic_Door) of Botanicheskiy Sad. They were not bloodthirsty beasts but sentient beings seeking peace and symbiosis with humanity. Metro citizens, misunderstanding them, fired on them out of fear — and the Dark Ones' very psychic presence drove people to madness, as revealed in *[The Gospel According to Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Gospel_According_to_Artyom)*.

The perceived threat is what first brings [Hunter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Ranger)) to VDNKh, launching Artyom's journey. Throughout the game the Dark Ones repeatedly try to make Artyom understand their intent, projecting peaceful visions of children playing or leaves on a tree. As a child, Artyom was once saved from mutants by a Dark One; through their brief psychic contact he became immune to their influence and was "adopted" as **the chosen one** — the bridge between the two races — though he later forgot the encounter.

At [Ostankino Tower](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ostankino_Tower), the Dark Ones desperately try to convey their message of peace. Artyom understands his purpose too late: the missiles destroy the Dark Ones' nest, leaving him conflicted. In the non-canonical **Enlightened** [ending](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Endings) (earned with enough [moral points](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moral_points)), Artyom can knock the missile guidance system from the tower and spare them; the canonical **Ranger** ending has the strike proceed.

## Metro: Last Light

Though seemingly wiped out, the Dark Ones return — chiefly as the [Baby Dark One](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Dark_One), sole survivor of the D6 strike. Targeted by the [Rangers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Rangers_of_the_Order), it escapes capture by [Nazis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nazis) and ends up in a [Hansa](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hansa) freak-circus. Red Line general [Korbut](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Korbut_(Character)), hoping to raise an army of domesticated Dark Ones, seeks to seize it. Artyom and [Khan](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Khan_(Character)) rescue the child; assigned to kill it, Artyom instead saves it and the two form a partnership. Together they discover D6's best-kept secret — a nest of hibernating Dark Ones. During the [battle for D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Metro_Last_Light_Level)), the outcome depends on the player's moral points: in the **Redemption** ending the Dark Ones return to annihilate the Red Army and save the Rangers. The Dark Ones ultimately declare they must leave humanity for a time, vowing to return.

## Metro Exodus

A Dark One is glimpsed on rooftops in [Novosibirsk](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Novosibirsk), an ideal habitat given its extreme radiation and near-total absence of humans. Its possible presence is implied when Miller inexplicably knows where to find Artyom. Other Easter-egg appearances include an infant Dark One by a campfire at the Caspian Sea and one seen outside the Aurora's window in the main menu after the odometer reaches 2033 kilometers.

## Metro 2039

The Dark Ones are set to return in *Metro 2039*.

## Morphology and Appearance

Aside from a broadly humanoid shape, Dark Ones have long, slender limbs, hands with four long digits, and dark ashen-grey skin. Adults have sunken lower jaws; juveniles have insect-like mandibles. They are highly resistant to radiation and cold, and possess extra-sensory perception — in *Last Light* depicted as sensing blood and aggression, with beings glowing red (angry), blue (fearful), yellow (ambivalent), or green (friendly). They can also perceive paranormal phenomena such as ghosts.

The Dark Ones cannot speak vocally but communicate **telepathically** and share a collective consciousness. Their powers are too strong for humans — uncontrolled contact can destroy a person's mind. Using these abilities they create realistic illusions and induce fear. They tell Artyom they no longer understand technology and approach humanity as partners: they would help mankind survive, and in return humans could reclaim the surface — though the novel leaves open whether this is true co-existence or a form of slavery. Khan theorizes they may have been genetically engineered before the war as radiation-immune telepathic soldiers, which would explain their long maturation and lack of visible reproductive organs; the presence of a navel, however, suggests live birth much like humans.

In combat the Dark Ones are stronger and more durable than humans but still fairly weak against firepower (roughly five [revolver](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Revolver) shots) — their psychic abilities are what make them truly dangerous.

## Trivia

- The very first Dark One encounter is actually a [ghost](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ghosts_(Phenomenon)), reliving its death at Hunter's hands.
- Children's drawings and a dying stalker's floor-etching both depict the tall humanoid figures, hinting the Dark Ones contacted others besides Artyom.
- In *[Metro 2033 Redux](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_Redux)*, the Dark Ones in [Dead City](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dead_City_1) turn invisible and can be revealed with the flashlight, like ghosts.
- "Homo Novus" is Latin for "new man."
- *[Metro 2035](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2035)* ignores the Dark Ones' *Last Light* plotline.
