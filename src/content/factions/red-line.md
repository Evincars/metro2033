# Red Line

> "One Party, One Leader, One Ideology."
> — [Pavel Morozov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Pavel_Morozov)

The **Red Line** (Russian: *Красная Линия*) is a [one-party socialist political union](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Factions) in post-apocalyptic [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow), based around the [Sokolnicheskaya Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sokolnicheskaya_Line), which serves as its artery. One of the most powerful factions in the Metro alongside [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza), the Red Line is in a state of permanent conflict with the much smaller but more militarily advanced fascist stations known as the [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich). By 2039, the Red Line has been absorbed by the Novoreich.

- **Base location:** [Sokolnicheskaya Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sokolnicheskaya_Line)
- **Leader:** [Maxim Moskvin](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Maxim_Moskvin)
- **Disposition towards the player:** Hostile
- **Allies:** [Revolutionaries](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Revolutionaries)
- **Enemies:** [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza) (intermittently), [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich), [Bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Faction)), [Spartan Rangers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spartan_Rangers)

## Overview

With the largest population in the Metro (estimated at 15,000), an unparalleled capacity for mass conscription, and a number of stations and metres of track surpassed only by Hansa, the Red Line is one of the most formidable factions in the post-apocalyptic world. Juxtaposed to Hanza's policy of political ambivalence and economic hegemony, the Red Line pursues a policy of violent expansion whenever possible.

The Red Line is a single-party state ruled by the Communist Party of the Moscow Underground and its General Secretary, [Maxim Moskvin](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Maxim_Moskvin). Following a Marxist-Leninist and Neo-Stalinist ideology, political repression and indoctrination are an everyday part of life for Line citizens, considered a vital tool if the Party is to maintain its ascendancy or prevent fascist infiltration.

## Etymology

The names used before the war to describe the different lines of the Underground are based on their colours on Metro maps, and Sokolnicheskaya was always depicted in bright red. This, combined with the pseudo-socialist nature of the Red Line and the association of the colour red with socialism, easily explains the post-apocalyptic origins of the name.

## History

### Formation

Sokolnicheskaya was built as a testament to the glory of the Soviet state and the power of Stalin himself, its walls sculpted with sumptuous statues and mosaics. This aura of Soviet majesty attracted everyone nostalgic for the socialist past who saw in the new world an opportunity to build a communist utopia. Following the collapse of [Central Metro Command](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Central_Metro_Command) a few years after the destruction of the surface, Communist agitators throughout the line brought their work to fruition. Muscovites overthrew their administrations and established revolutionary councils, and the masterminds of the revolution declared a first "Interstational" with the goal of uniting the entire Metro and establishing true Communism for all time.

### Attempts at Expansion

Following the conquest of almost the entire Sokol Branch, the Red Line seemed on the verge of taking over the entire Metro. However, a coalition of stations led by Hanza — which viewed the revolution as a threat to its economic interests — readied itself. The resulting [Hansa-Red Line War](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hansa_-_Red_Line_War) became a bloody stalemate. As mutinies, desertions, and growing unrest mounted, the two parties reached an agreement: Ploshchad Revolyutsii was ceded to the Reds from the [Arbat Confederation](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Arbat_Confederation), while Arbat received the Biblioteka Imeni Lenina station (later ceded to Polis). A guarantee of free transit was made by both parties, paving the way for a relationship of mutual economic dependency and watchful ambivalence.

### Conquered by Novoreich

By 2039, the Red Line has been conquered by the Novoreich.

## Territory

Following many conflicts over territory, the Red Line actively began trading consumer goods with Hansa and has begun the electrification of tunnels, though there are famines from time to time in Communist stations. Although their territorial possessions allow the Red Line to produce a large amount of food and weaponry, its conflicts with the Reich have drained these resources substantially.

## Government

### Ideology

The Red Line's state ideology is a far-left mixture of [Trotskyism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyism) and [Stalinism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalinism). They claim to promote class equality, common ownership, and anti-capitalism. On paper, everything that comes from labour is shared equally, meaning all are entitled to food, water, and housing. As noted by [Pavel Morozov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Pavel_Morozov), their society follows a strict mindset — one leader, one party, and one ideology — with order and control considered more important than individual freedoms.

In reality, life on the Red Line stations is tough for most residents, with a brutal police-state system of government and frequent shortages. Citizens are often overworked, underpaid, and terrorized by the government. Those who disrespect a soldier or are simply in the wrong place at the wrong time are arrested and sent to [Lubyanka](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Lubyanka) station, used as a political prison; a common phrase holds that "the ticket to Lubyanka is always one-way". These policies cause large numbers of [refugees](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Refugees) to flee the Red Line.

### Disposition

Officially, the Red Line is neutral to most other factions, the exceptions being the Fourth Reich and [Bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits). In the novel, the Red Line also has an alliance with the [Revolutionaries](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Revolutionaries). Despite officially being an anti-imperialist and neutral state, the Red Line is possibly the most imperialistic state in the Metro, often attempting to gain control of nearby stations by exerting influence or backing the overthrow of their leadership in favour of a pro-communist government.

## Red Army

### Strength

The Red Army, the official military of the Red Line, is believed to be the largest army in the Metro due to the Line's much higher population to conscript from — which also means lower standards toward the combat capability of their troops. They have the manpower to launch costly human-wave attacks against the Nazi defenses. Soldiers are promised payment of one full clip of bullets for fighting but are pressured into spending their pay on the enemy. The [Faction Pack](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Faction_Pack) reveals the Red Army also has a skilled [Sniper Team](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sniper_Team), and the Line contains a number of double agents and spies commanded by head of intelligence [General Korbut](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Korbut_(Character)), with some such as [Lesnitsky](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Lesnitsky) infiltrating the [Rangers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Rangers).

### Equipment

The majority of front-line soldiers are poorly trained and equipped conscripts fresh from the stations, usually armed only with a [Bastard](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bastard) carbine, a single magazine, and a non-protective cloth uniform, commonly used as cannon fodder. Despite this, many Communist soldiers can be seen wearing professional combat armour and metal helmets, armed with [Kalashes](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kalash_(AK-74M)) and [night vision goggles](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Night_vision_goggles) — likely officers or elite units. By 2034 the Red Army appears greatly developed, employing laser sights, optics, [heavy armoured suits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Armor#Universal_Heavy_Combat_Armor), and CBRN suits. During the assault on [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6), they field [ballistic shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_shield), [flamethrowers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Flamethrower), [gatling guns](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Gatling), improvised "[tanks](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line_Tank)", and an [armored train](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Armored_train).

### Attitude

The demeanor of Red Army soldiers varies greatly. Some genuinely believe in Communism, some participate as a family tradition, and others are purely in it for the money. The lack of morale is shown when soldiers refuse to sing the national anthem during a commissar's rallying speech. The Red Line's officers maintain a very high discipline standard, executing a person for "desertion" even if they were attempting to benefit the Red Line's cause — as seen in "Frontline", where a soldier pleads for his life before being executed by his officer as part of a "Revolutionary Tribunal".

## Appearances

### Metro 2033

The Red Line doesn't play a crucial role in the events of [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Video_Game)), being featured briefly. They are only fought against in the level "Frontline", where they are already fighting against the Reich, and can be entirely avoided if Artyom sneaks by them.

### Metro: Last Light

The Red Line returns as a major faction and is the most common human enemy in the game. The Communists absconded with several pre-war biological weapons, which party leaders see fit to use on various Metro stations to take control. The Red Line has secretly developed a huge army, mobilized against the Rangers at [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6) and against the Fourth Reich from a [second front](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Heavy_Squad). On both fronts, the Red Line suffers massive losses and none of their objectives are achieved.

### Metro 2035

Before the events of the book, the Red Line attacks D6; they are repelled but cost the Order half their men. At the start of the book, the Red Line is facing a famine due to the mushroom disease that has swept the Metro. Amid the conflict with the Reich around Teatralnaya, [Bessolov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Aleksei_Bessolov) later reveals that the Red Line, like all the other major factions, is under the control of the [Watchers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Invisible_Watchers).

### Metro Exodus

After the defeat of Cheslav Korbut's battalion at [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Metro_Last_Light_Level)), the general is put under tribunal and Secretary General Moskvin enters into an alliance with the Order and Polis. At a Hanza radio station, Artyom can overhear soldiers noting that the Hanza government could withdraw weapons for an operation against the Reds or the Reich.

## Trivia

- The only person to ever specifically mention the Soviet Union is [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller), while inside D6, referring to the technology as having "Soviet soul".
- The war between the [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich) and the Red Line is an allusion to the conflict of the Eastern Front in World War II.
- New recruits are paid a single fully loaded magazine of [military-grade rounds](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/5.45x39mm) upon joining, but are expected to use it on the enemy.
- The Red Line and the Fourth Reich are the only two factions to possess tanks.
- The armoured train used by the Red Line is possibly a reference to Leon Trotsky's armoured train.
- The Red Line most likely uses the Russian version of the Internationale as a national anthem; the commissar at the start of "Frontline" and Pavel defiantly sing it while being tortured.
- On the Red Line banner, "CCCP" and "Пролетарии всех стран, соединяйтесь!" ("Proletarians of all countries, Unite!") orbit a globe defaced by the hammer and sickle, suggesting the official name may be the "Union of Soviet Socialist Republics".
- In *Metro: Last Light*, Artyom is initially led to believe the Reds are allies and the Nazis the primary antagonists; once Pavel betrays Artyom, the Reds are revealed as the game's primary antagonists.
