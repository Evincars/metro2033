# Savage Cannibals of the Great Worm Cult

The **Savage Cannibals of the Great Worm Cult** (Russian: Червепоклонники, literally "Worm-worshipers") are followers of a xenophobic and technophobic society mostly comprised of brainwashed children. They appear in the [novel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)) and in *[Metro Awakening](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Awakening)*. They mainly reside at [Park Pobedy](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Park_Pobedy) and at both mouths of [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)).

## Overview

The cult is first encountered in the novel when a boy from [Kievskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya), Oleg (the son of Anton), is kidnapped to be integrated into the cult. When [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) goes searching for the boy, he too is captured and learns of the malicious religion.

The Great Worm Cult formed after a number of estranged cultists kidnapped and brainwashed children from several stations (notably Kievskaya) to serve their god, the Great Worm. According to the myth told to the children, the Great Worm was a deity that created the tunnels of the Metro by burrowing, formed humans from its stomach, and then descended toward the center of the earth, to return in a millennium.

The primitive cultists are fiercely xenophobic and technophobic, considering anyone who enters their territory to be "People of Machines" — enemies fit only for cannibalization. These beliefs are rooted in those of their priests, who blame the [War of 2013](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/World_War_III) on technology. This makes the isolationist cultists highly superstitious and fearful of traveling into D6 on certain days of the week, so as not to encroach on the Great Worm's privacy — or, perhaps more likely, the activity of the creature at the [Kremlin](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kremlin).

The cult follows a notable hierarchy: the children follow elderly priests and a lesser second class of hypnotists, who try to convert those they encounter to the way of the Great Worm. While one hypnotist had a decent effect on Melnik and the [Stalkers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Stalkers) during their rescue and approach to D6, Oleg proved resistant to hypnotism.

By the end of 2033 the cult seems all but eliminated. Its members are fought off and its leaders captured, only for them to deliver nihilistic confessions and warnings about the evil in D6 before committing suicide. During the rescue team's retreat, after saving Artyom and Anton, they cover their escape by throwing a grenade. This indirectly causes a supporting column to fall, trapping the followers of the Great Worm Cult and likely leaving them to starve.

## Culture

After years of isolation, the cannibals have developed their own culture, holding celebrations with dancing, singing, vodka, and prayers to their deity.

All members are bald except the priests, who alone are allowed to keep their hair. Each member has a worm-shaped line painted on their forehead, seemingly indicating rank — the leader bears a distinct red one, with a blue variant also mentioned though most depictions show white. Most walk almost fully nude and are of small stature and sickly appearance. Due to radiation, some have additional fingers while others possess mind-altering abilities through their eyes; it is implied some are blind or otherwise deformed.

Their armament consists solely of blowpipes — long, narrow tubes made from insulation — through which they fire poisoned darts, along with long bayonets. Because of sickness the cult's own children are too weak, so they kidnap children from other stations. They likewise kidnap adults, whom they cannibalize, preparing them by putting them in bags and beating them to death to cause hematomas. Their doctrine speaks against cannibalism, but it is viewed as a temporary necessary evil.

The cult follows a monotheistic religion distantly similar to Christianity. Their sole deity, the "Great Worm," is said to have existed since time itself and to have carved the world from stone thousands of years ago. In their lore, the Great Worm first gave birth to creatures like himself, but they all died of thirst; because of this the Great Worm cried, creating water. He then created creatures again — first fleas, then chickens, pigs, cats, dogs, and eventually humans, whom he granted sentience. Some tunnels of the Metro are viewed as sacred, and entering them on specific days is believed to bring curses or death.

## Trivia

- Despite the cult leader explaining the falsehoods of the Great Worm to the Stalkers, Artyom is shocked by a large unknown moving object behind a door in D6. Even after Pavel suggests it may be a passing drilling rig, doubts remain — leaving the existence of a real Great Worm a mystery. It could be the [Metro 2](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2) automatic train, still running after all these years.
- The Great Worm Children speak a very simplified form of Russian, probably due to their isolation.
- The Church of the Water Tsar in *Metro Exodus* is partly inspired by the Worm Cult, with a fish replacing the Worm and a similar fear of technology, though the cannibalism aspect is shifted to the Yamantau bunker dwellers.
- *Metro Awakening* is the only game to feature the Worm Cult, where they serve as the antagonists of the final two chapters — and the existence of the Great Worm is finally confirmed to be real.
