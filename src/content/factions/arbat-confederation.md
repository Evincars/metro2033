# Arbat Confederation

The **Arbat Confederation** is a group of stations composed of [Kievskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya), its adjoining stations, and — apparently at one time — [Park Pobedy](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Park_Pobedy). The Confederation is mentioned in the [novel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)) *Metro 2033*, but not in the [video game](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Videogame)).

- **Base location:** [Kievskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya)-radial
- **Disposition towards the player:** Neutral
- **Allies:** [Stalkers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Stalkers), [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza)
- **Enemies:** [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) (previously), [Great Worm Cult](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Savage_Cannibals_of_the_Great_Worm_Cult)

## Overview

The Arbat Confederation is a moderate capitalist autocracy, primarily governed by immigrants from the Caucasus, non-Russian nationality traders, and their descendants. It was once formidable and influential, yet by the year 2033 it is composed of only three stations.

Frequent attacks from [Mutants](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Mutants) led to stations like [Park Pobedy](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Park_Pobedy), and those nearby, being abandoned and sealed off with controlled tunnel collapses. This gave the [Great Worm Cult](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Savage_Cannibals_of_the_Great_Worm_Cult) a perfect opportunity to set up their home close by, and frequent attacks and kidnappings ensued. The secret hatch the cultists used to reach the tunnels surrounding the Confederation was eventually blocked off, but the stations stayed abandoned. This most likely attracted a high number of [Stalkers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Stalkers), Diggers, and Scavengers to the Confederation, making the Arbat Confederation one of the largest hotspots in the Moscow Metropolitan for those who traverse the tunnels and dare venture to the surface, as well as many others with shady backgrounds. The constant atmosphere of a bustling bazaar, and its acceptance of those of non-Russian backgrounds, drew many people — and despite now being a satellite of [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza) with only a fraction of its former military power, it remains a hotspot for dwellers of various ethnicities.

## History

After the bombs fell, many immigrants from the eastern republics of the late USSR took cover in the Moscow Metropolitan, primarily those with a Caucasus background (countries like Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, etc.).

During the [Hansa – Red Line War](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hansa_-_Red_Line_War), the Confederation was allied with [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza). Although they gained the station of the Lenin Library (later given to Polis), their forces were severely weakened and the population decreased over the years. With the unseen threat from [Park Pobedy](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Park_Pobedy) to [Kievskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya) only disposed of in recent years, people have been fleeing the station, leading the Arbat Confederation to install a soft quarantine regime on the Kievskaya-radial (the blue line). Now the Arbat Confederation is nothing more than a weak Hansa satellite, dependent on their resources and protection.
