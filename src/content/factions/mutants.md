# Mutants

**Mutants** are organisms whose DNA has been altered by radiation or chemical exposure. Appearing throughout both the [novels](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Book_Series) and the [games](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Video_Game_Series), they are usually fierce and dangerous, attacking any human they encounter — though some are neutral or even intelligent beings that try to communicate. Areas infested by mutants are marked with a biohazard symbol on most Metro maps.

## Overview

Common mutants are tough creatures adapted to the nuclear environment, able to withstand heavy physical trauma. Derived from local fauna, many display above-normal intelligence and hunt in packs. Most are shaped for life in darkness. The majority belong to species with high reproductive rates — such as the numerous [lurkers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Lurkers) — which helps explain how thoroughly the surviving fauna mutated in just twenty years.

## Mutant Animals

- **[Bear](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bear)** — a radiation-warped bear grown to monstrous size; a powerful boss entity.
- **[Biomass](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Biomass_(Mutant))** — a huge mass of radioactive organic material formed after the use of "Ancient Weapons."
- **[Blind Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Blind_Ones)** — giant gorilla-like mutants in the [Novosibirsk](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Novosibirsk) research facility, possibly related to librarians.
- **[Corpse Eaters](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Corpse_Eaters)** — non-lethal scavengers that feed on the dead, including their own kind.
- **[Dark Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones)** — powerful sentient telepathic humanoids, also known as Homo Novus.
- **[Demon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Demon)** — a giant bat-like creature found only on the surface.
- **[Giant Amoeba](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Giant_Amoeba)** — an explosive ball of protoplasm that detonates on contact.
- **[Humanimal](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Humanimal)** — once-humans regressed to an animalistic state that attack their human cousins.
- **[Librarian](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Librarian)** — quasi-intelligent, gorilla-like beasts found in the [library](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Library_(Level)); common and black varieties.
- **[Lurker](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Lurker)** — small, agile creatures likely mutated from rodents.
- **[Mutant Crow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Mutant_Crow)** — small flying bird-like surface animals.
- **[Nosalis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nosalis)** — common mole-derived mutants ("upyrs" in the books) with six varieties in *Metro 2033*.
- **[Nosalis Rhino](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nosalis_Rhino)** — a massive alpha-female nosalis leader.
- **[Shrimp](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Shrimp)** — large chitinous marine mutants of the metro "river" and surface swamps.
- **[Snakes](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Snake_(Mutant))** — reptiles that have developed bug-like legs and tail pincers.
- **[Spiderbug](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spiderbug)** — scorpion/spider-like arthropods deathly vulnerable to light.
- **[Tsar Fish](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Tsar_Fish)** — a giant aquatic mutant of the [Volga](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Volga).
- **[Watcher](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Watcher)** — dark-furred pack mutants known for their distinctive howling.
- **[Wolf](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Wolf)** — a pack animal largely unchanged by radiation.
- **[Worm](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Worm)** — giant leeches in a flooded Novosibirsk station.
- **[Reaper](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Reaper)** — a towering creature with long sickle-clawed forelimbs and keen hearing.
- **[Nops](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nops)** — heavily mutated dogs.

## Plant Mutants

- **[Liana](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Liana)** — large tentacles hanging from building interiors.
- **[Nettle](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nettle)** — poison-gas bushes found in the Volga area.
- **[Radioactive Luminescent Mushrooms](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Radioactive_Luminescent_Mushroom)** — common fungi that cause or feed off radiation.
- **[Spore Bud](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spore_Bud)** — an inactive, harmless plant species.

## Supernatural Phenomena

- **[Anomaly](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anomaly_(Phenomenon))** — bright blue balls of light that electrify and kill everything nearby.
- **[Damned Souls](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Damned_Souls)** — malevolent dead that drag down anyone within reach.
- **[Ghosts](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ghosts_(Phenomenon))** — after-images of those who died in the metro and have not passed on.
- **[Nagornaya Phantoms](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nagornaya_Phantoms)** — fog-shrouded entities that kill the youngest of a group.
- **[Master of the Tunnels](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Master_of_the_Tunnels)** — a disembodied keeper of the St. Petersburg Metro from folklore.

## Other Creatures

Many non-mutated animals also survive throughout the Metro, including bats, cats, chickens, [dogs](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dog), [pigs](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Pigs) (a dietary staple raised on mushrooms), rats, [deer](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Deer), [hares](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hare), [frogs](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Frog), and largely unchanged crows and insects. Cultivated plants include mushrooms (the basis for [Shroom Vodka](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Shroom_Vodka) and other products), moss, cucumbers, potatoes, and tomatoes grown in hydroponic farms.

## In-Game Notes

Most mutants are unorganized and treated as animals, though many form pack structures and will attack both humans and rival mutant species. On rare occasions humans and mutants cooperate — as when Artyom and a [watchman](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Watchmen) pack together fend off the mutant [bear](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bear). The [librarians](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Librarians) are among the few potentially sentient mutants, known to set traps for [stalkers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Stalkers) and even grow plants.
