# Hanza (Hanseatic League)

The **Commonwealth of the Stations of the Ring Line**, or the Hanseatic League (more commonly known as Hansa, Hanza, Hanse or even Ganza, ГАНЗА), is a trading alliance federation comprising several different stations, making up a large area of the Metro. Economically and militarily, Hansa is undoubtedly the most powerful faction of the [Moscow Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_Metro), keeping tight control of the underground's [Ring Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Koltsevaya_Line).

- **Base location:** [The Ring Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Koltsevaya_Line)
- **Leader:** [Valeri Loginov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Loginov), General Manager
- **Disposition towards the player:** Neutral (*Metro 2033*, *Metro: Last Light*); Hostile (*Metro Exodus*)
- **Allies:** [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis), [Arbat Confederation](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Arbat_Confederation), [1905 Confederation](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/1905_Confederation), [Belorusskaya Trade Outpost](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Belorusskaya_Trade_Outpost), [Baumansky Alliance](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Baumansky_Alliance), [VDNKh Commonwealth](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/VDNKh_Commonwealth), [Sevastopolskaya HPP](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sevastopolskaya_HPP), [Rangers of the Order](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spartan_Rangers) (2035)
- **Enemies:** [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) (previously), [Bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Faction)), [Aurora Crew](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Aurora#Crew)

## Overview

Upon formation and acquiring a decent foothold in the Metro, Hansa quickly developed into the Metro's economic powerhouse, a hyper-capitalist utopia for the rich. It's one of the few places in the Metro where public transportation, running water, and large-scale electric lighting are widespread. They usually make "partnerships" with stations outside of the Ring (such as the [Arbat Confederation](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Arbat_Confederation) and [Belorusskaya Trade Outpost](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Belorusskaya_Trade_Outpost)), causing those stations to become resource-stations dependent on Hanza. While not dependent on Hansa for resources, both the [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich) and [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) maintain trade relationships with Hanza.

Hanza and [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis) seem the most democratic factions within the Metro; many citizens from the Fourth Reich and Red Line expressed wanting to move to Hanza or Polis. However, both the Communists and Nazis have no tolerance for refugees, and those lucky enough to arrive at a Hanseatic station are often turned away due to Hansa's suspicion of outsiders.

Hansa is well-militarized to protect its wealth; all its stations are heavily guarded, it boasts motor-transportation and [flamethrowers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Flamethrower), and its militia is outfitted with high-end military-grade weapons. Hanseatic stations also appear austere when it comes to entering and leaving stations, with non-Hansa personnel requiring passports or other forms of identification, as well as having their weapons confiscated until they leave. The Commonwealth has also managed to salvage an incredible amount of pre-war technology, including plastic quarantine sheeting, metal detectors, working computers, medical scanning equipment, and biohazard gear.

## History

The Commonwealth of the Stations of the Ring Line emerged shortly after central control disbanded. Crossing all other lines, these stations were vital points of trade routes. They quickly became the place for all traders, becoming incredibly rich in a short amount of time. Soon they understood their wealth would become the envy of others and took the only possible decision: they formed the Commonwealth. Its official name is rarely used because people were quick to title them "Hanza" after the Hanseatic League, an alliance of trading cities in the Middle Ages.

At first, Hansa included only part of the Ring Line stations; the unification was not instantaneous. The so-called "Northern Arc" included at first stations from [Kievskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya) to [Prospekt Mira](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Market_Station_(Location)), while the other fraction consisted of Kurskaya, Taganskaya, and Oktyabrskaya. Later Paveletskaya and Dobryninskaya joined Hansa, and the "Southern Arc" emerged. But the key obstacle to joining this ring of power was the Sokolnicheskaya Line (renamed Red Line). This led to one of the Metro's major conflicts, the [war between Hansa and the Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hansa-_Red_Line_War).

The war ended with a mutually beneficial agreement signed by the Red Line and Hansa, resulting in the acquisition of the Lenin Library Station by Polis and the acquisition of the Ploschad Revolutsii (Revolution Square) Station by the Red Line. Hansa, however, benefited more, as the agreement meant they occupied every station on the Ring Line, allowing its traders to quickly navigate the Metro and forcing outside traders to pay for passage. Despite that unequal exchange, the Red Line happily accepted the deal, as it granted them easy access to Red Square and the monuments of old Russia, which the Red Line leadership revered greatly. Shortly thereafter, Hansa made a worthy trade partner in the [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line), despite the previous conflict.

## Ideology

Like the many factions of the Metro, Hansa have their own ideology. They are modeled after the United States from 1900–1960, a society wherein regulations on the free market are little to none and everybody has a right to make it for themselves. While this can be looked upon with pleasure, it also gives off an "every man for himself" vibe, something the Red Line fears. Despite being a free-market economy, they are not an entirely libertarian society; in the *Metro 2033* novel, Artyom notes that they have recently outlawed marijuana.

### Notable Hanseatic Stations

Hanseatic stations are noted as being freer and less militant than others; they, however, have other problems. Its stations are all on some front, considering they surround every major faction. As for territory, each station on the Ring Line belongs to Hansa; the most notable are:

- [Kievskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kievskaya) (in part — the other two sections are under the [Arbat Confederation](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Arbat_Confederation))
- [Prospekt Mira](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Prospekt_Mira)
- [Oktyabrskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Oktyabrskaya) (in part — the other section is independently run)

## In the games

### Metro 2033

In [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Video_Game)), [Bourbon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bourbon) and other characters mention Hansa early in the game, but Hansa itself is rarely encountered. [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) first encounters Hansa in the level "[Bridge](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bridge_(Metro_2033_Level))", when an armoured railcar is on the other platform looking for [bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits). Hansa is finally given a full presence in the level "[Market](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Market_(Level))", where Hansa's militia guards the markets and stations for arriving travellers. This is the last time Hansa appears in *Metro 2033*.

### Metro: Last Light

Hansa carries a similar presence in [Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Last_Light), remaining largely passive. Hansa remains one of the three powers vying for [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)), though doing so in a less militaristic manner, threatening economic sanctions on the [Rangers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Rangers). As a result, the Rangers don't view Hansa as a hostile force or direct enemy. In fact, during the level "[Quarantine](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Quarantine)", both the Rangers and Hansa's police work together to contain the infection at Oktyabrskaya, while Hansa takes in the refugees from that station. In the [Faction Pack DLC](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Faction_Pack_DLC) mission [Kshatriya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kshatriya_(Level)), the Ranger Trainee originally came from a Hanseatic station.

### Metro Exodus

Hansa (assisted in secret by the [Spartan Order](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Rangers_of_the_Order)) had built a series of radio jammers that prevent any signals from coming in or out of the city. This was ostensibly done to protect Moscow from being nuked again, or otherwise invaded, by NATO forces. Any outsiders that managed to reach Moscow were picked up by Hansa surface patrols and subsequently executed as "enemy spies". It is, however, later learned by the Spartans' leader, [Colonel Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller), that the threat of NATO attack had been a complete fabrication — the radio jammers had actually been set up to keep the people of the Metro isolated and trapped underground. Both Hansa and the Spartan Order had been deceived by the [Invisible Watchers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Invisible_Watchers), a mysterious group secretly controlling all the major factions within the Metro for their own benefit.

Two Spartan Rangers, Artyom and [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Character)), discovered one of Hansa's patrol trains. A Hansa surface patrol picked up the Spartans, mistook them for spies, and intended to execute them alongside two captured survivors. The Rangers fought their way out, damaging the radio-jammer system and stealing one of Hansa's trains. A second, more heavily armoured train was sent to retake the first; Artyom boarded and blew it up. Understanding their actions would mean Hansa would arrest and execute them as traitors, the Spartans who hijacked the patrol train were forced to leave the Metro, and Moscow, behind.

## Metro 2035

In *Metro 2035*, Hanza has become closer allies with the Spartan Rangers and helped to restore the Order's ranks by sending some of its own finest soldiers as candidates for new Ranger recruits — following the costly [Battle for D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_for_D6) that was fought against the Red Line. The book also reveals more information regarding their leadership, as well as shedding some light on the origins of the [Invisible Watchers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Invisible_Watchers) "myth".

## Trivia

- "Hansa" is a term that often refers to the Hanseatic League, an alliance of various German trading cities and merchant guilds that lasted from the 13th to 17th centuries.
- The word itself is an old German term meaning simply 'group' or 'company'. It's used in a commercial context to this day in the name of the biggest German airline corporation, Lufthansa (lit. 'air company').
- In *Metro: Last Light*, Anna refers to Hansa as Koltsavaya, the real-life name of the Ring Line.
- Hansa is the only faction not to have its flag appear in-game.
- Hansa is portrayed very much like the United States during its isolationist policy before and during the first years of the Second World War — it tries to maintain neutral relationships with the other factions, refuses to take military action without provocation, and prefers to use economic sanctions and trade to help or harm other factions.
