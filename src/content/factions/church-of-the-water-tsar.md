# Church of the Water Tsar

**Church of the Water Tsar** (Russian: Церковь Водного Царя), commonly referred to as **Fanatics**, is a [faction](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Factions) in *[Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus)*. They are hostile to [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) and the [Aurora](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Aurora) crew, and are led by [Silantius](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Silantius).

## Overview

The Church of the Water Tsar is a religious cult that worships the [Tsar Fish](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Tsar_Fish), a monstrously large aquatic mutant inhabiting the [Volga](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Volga), believing it carries their dead to the afterlife. They are extremely technophobic and view the use of electric appliances as a mortal sin.

While they do trade with certain outsiders, they view most with suspicion, if not outright hostility. In order to join their ranks, one must renounce all technology, which is then either destroyed or stored in a nearby terminal to be "purified" by the Tsar Fish. If a cult member breaks the rules, they are usually punished by being forced to do dangerous or even suicidal jobs, such as exorcising [electric demons](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anomaly_(Phenomenon)).

Their attitude and views are largely shaped by [Silantius](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Silantius), their charismatic leader, and enforced by his paladins. However, during the ending cutscene of the Volga level, Silantius' dialogue with [Duke](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Duke) and Artyom suggests that he himself does not actually believe that using technology is a sin -- he created and maintains the cult simply to control the local population.

## Role in Metro Exodus

The Church of the Water Tsar appears in [The Volga](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Volga_(Metro_Exodus_Level)) level of *Metro Exodus*. The cult controls the railway bridge over the Volga, which the [Aurora](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Aurora) crew must cross. Their technophobia and hostility toward outsiders make them an immediate obstacle.

The cult's followers patrol the surrounding area, and their paladins serve as enforcers. Artyom must navigate through their territory while deciding whether to take a lethal or non-lethal approach. The player's moral choices in dealing with the cult members affect [Duke](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Duke)'s fate and the game's ending.

The cult also clashes with [bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Faction)) in the region, adding another layer of conflict to the Volga chapter.

## Trivia

- The cult makes an exception for ships on the Volga, probably because they collect tolls from those crossing their bridge.
- Silantius' hypocrisy is revealed late in the level -- he is aware that technology is not actually sinful but uses the belief as a tool of control.
- The Tsar Fish they worship is one of the largest mutants encountered in the Metro series.
