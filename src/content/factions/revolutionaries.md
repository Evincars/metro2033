# Revolutionaries

> "No, just simple communists."
> — Comrade Bonsai

The **Revolutionaries**, or **Trotskyists**, are a group of communists based at Avtozavodskaya who wage guerrilla warfare against the [fascists](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich), constantly harassing them.

## Overview

- **Base location:** Avtozavodskaya
- **Disposition toward player:** Neutral
- **Allies:** [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line)
- **Enemies:** [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich)

## Overview

The Revolutionaries stick with Trotsky's line of thinking, which is communist-oriented and follows the ideology of Che Guevara. Trotskyists believe that communist revolutions should be permanent, meaning that every country — or, in the Revolutionaries' case, every station — should revolt one after another with the support of other like-minded individuals, creating global communism. This differs from the Stalinism that holds all attention should be on one state, which for the [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) and the USSR is themselves alone. They are "simple communists", as said by Comrade Bonsai, meaning they are not from the Red Line. Their flag (or rag, as described by [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom)) is red with a picture of Che Guevara.

> "We have remained true to the revolutionary idea, unlike the collaborationist comrade Moskvin. Us comrades, we condemn them and their line."
> — Comrade Rusakov

Revolutionary bases are places where the Revolutionaries refuel and get repairs. Little is known about them, aside from the fact that they should not be well-known; thus they are hidden in convenient dead-ends, but they must be at least sparsely populated and well-equipped for repairs. The location of the auxiliary base — Avtozavodskaya (Zamoskvoretskaya Line), right after [Paveletskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Paveletskaya) — is given in the [novel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Novel)); a not-for-everybody place, because many people already fear going to Paveletskaya. The main base is generally considered to be on the Arbatsko–Pokrovskaya Line, behind the "[Baumansky Alliance](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Baumansky_Alliance)" stations, on Partizanskaya — another convenient and faraway dead-end. Handy repairs from the leftist Bauman Alliance, as well as a free ride, make this the most probable location.

## In the Novel

In the novel, the Revolutionaries are a left-ideological confederation that trades with Hansa and the Reds (for fuel) and are at war with the [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich). Eventually, [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) is saved from the Fourth Reich by the Revolutionaries before he is to be hanged as punishment for killing one of their officers. The Revolutionaries rescued Artyom because information had arrived that the fascists were planning some kind of atrocity, so they went to stop it.

Later, in *[Metro 2034](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2034)*, Avtozavodskaya is shown as a Trotskyist station with its own particular uprising history. Sasha and her father were exiled to Kolomenskaya after the communist uprising. They still regularly trade with Sasha and her father, however, as her father brings valuable artifacts from the surface. After Sasha is rescued by Hunter and Homer, they go to the sealed door of Avtozavodskaya and wait for it to open. When it does, they are confronted by three Trotskyists on a motorized trolley who are swiftly gunned down by Hunter. While stealing their protective suits, Hunter is terrified when he sees that one of the dead men is black, as it reminds him of the Dark Ones. The trio take the motorized trolley and speed through Avtozavodskaya before the alarm can be raised.

It is possible that during the Fourth Reich's uprising, the non-Russian survivors at [Pushkinskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Pushkinskaya), and even at [Chekhovskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Chekhovskaya), managed to escape to the western lines of the Moscow Metro. It is a fact that Comrade Maxim is African and Bonsai is an Asian man.

> "Besides, it's our mission — to bother them constantly."
> — Comrade Bonsai

Artyom only gets to know a few people from this group; he comes to know Comrade Rusakov, Comrade Bonsai, Comrade Maxim, Comrade Fyodor, and their dog, called Karatsyupa. Comrade Rusakov is the leader of this squad.

## Owned Metro Lines

The Revolutionaries own only a single metro station, Avtozavodskaya, which is also their main base, located between [Paveletskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Paveletskaya) and Kolomenskaya. Not much is said about their station, and they typically keep it sealed off with their hermetic seal. To reach Kolomenskaya from Avtozavodskaya, one has to travel via a metro bridge above the surface and then underground again, since the Technopark station and its surrounding tunnels collapsed into an abyss when the bombs fell. This is done via the motorized trolley kept at Avtozavodskaya, which the Revolutionaries used to trade with the people living at Kolomenskaya, or with handcarts. All of the other tunnels on the south side of Avtozavodskaya were thought to be sealed, though some people did live in them, and Hunter was able to find a way on foot to access the station from them.

## Trivia

- They also call themselves the First International Red Fighting Brigade of the Moscow Metropolitan in the name of Ernesto Che Guevara.
- In the book, they use the "No pasarán" motto (They will not pass), popularised during the Siege of Madrid in the Spanish Civil War.
- The legend for the Revolutionaries on the book's map is a star, which could be associated with the star Che Guevara wore on his beret.
- They might have some contacts with the Red Line, as they receive fuel from them.
- They are not encountered during the game, but the Red soldiers at the beginning of "[Frontline](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Frontline_(Metro_2033_Level))" closely resemble the Revolutionaries in the novel.
- The Revolutionaries call each other "Comrades", a common moniker among communists.
- The appearance of the Revolutionaries may refer to Leon Trotsky's armored train, in which he travelled through Russia during the Russian Civil War (1917–1922).
