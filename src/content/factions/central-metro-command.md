# Central Metro Command

**Central Metro Command** was an emergency government organization hastily assembled in the immediate aftermath of [World War III](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/World_War_III) in an attempt to bring order to the survivors who had fled into the [Moscow Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Post-Apocalyptic_Metro_System), before it disbanded.

## Overview

When the air raid sirens sounded over [Moscow](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow) around July 2013, tens of thousands of Russians fled into the Metro — estimates range from 40,000 to 250,000 who originally sought shelter, though the numbers are now far fewer after years of war and mutant attacks. Among them were soldiers of the Russian Federation, high-ranking officials, policemen, and politicians who had direct access to the Metro from their government buildings. Seen as figures of power, they took control of the refugees and tried to organize them.

Many Metro dwellers believed the President and his cabinet had fled to secret bunkers behind the Ural Mountains and would one day return to rebuild the country — a rumor possibly propagated by Metro Command to maintain morale, or one based on genuine contact with the President before all outside communication was lost.

It is unclear how effective Central Metro Command truly was, but within a few years — as it became clear no rescue was coming and the surface remained irradiated — the command structure broke down. The unified Metro collapsed into isolated city-stations that grew wary of one another, and fighting broke out. Central Command's remnants held on as long as possible in [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)), after which any semblance of pre-war Russia was gone.

### Legacy

Though long dispersed by the events of *[Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro:_Last_Light)*, Metro Command is referenced several times. Characters such as [Su](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Su) and [Bar](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bar) state that after the bombs fell, pre-war scientists sealed themselves inside [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)).

## Trivia

- It is unknown whether the [Rangers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Rangers) found any bodies inside D6 when they captured it; the remaining scientists may have been devoured by the [biomass](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Biomass_(Mutant)).
- Many government buildings, such as the [Kremlin](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kremlin), connected not to the regular Metro but to the secret [Metro 2](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2) tunnels. Their survivors may have organized separately, but by 2033 most had died or rejoined the regular Metro, as no one lives in Metro 2 aside from D6.
- It is possible that Central Metro Command became — or had always been — the [Invisible Watchers](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Invisible_Watchers), taking secret, indirect control of the entire Metro after its supposed collapse.
