# Invisible Watchers

The **"Invisible Watchers"**, or the Invisible/Unseen Observers as they are called in the novel, are a [group](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Factions) of legendary, god-like figures in the Metro. In reality, they are remnants of the Russian Government responsible for manipulating the events of the entire Metro series for the sake of population control and influence over the people. The Watchers serve as the main antagonists of the franchise, with most of the events that transpire between the games and novels attributed back to the organisation.

- **Base location:** [Military installation GO-42](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bunker_42) (near Taganskaya station)
- **Leader:** [Aleksei Bessolov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Aleksei_Bessolov)

## Overview

The Invisible Watchers are a legend among the Metro inhabitants. According to the legend, they roam [Metro-2](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)), observing and directing the Metro inhabitants' every move. A Nazi soldier can be heard talking about Metro-2 and the Invisible Watchers in the [Black Station](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Black_Station_(Level)) level, and [Ulman](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ulman) pulls a joke about them on [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) and [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller) while in D6.

## The Legend

The in-game legend, recounted by a Nazi soldier at the Black Station, frames the Watchers in mythological terms. It tells that during a "Ragnarok" — the last battle of the gods — the Soviet Pantheon would take shelter in Metro-2. The ordinary Metro was made for "the sheep" (its inhabitants), while Metro-2 was made for "the shepherds" (the Watchers) and their dogs. Though the gate to Metro-2 was said to be closed forever, the legend holds that it never stopped existing — its tunnels winding alongside the Metro's own, its stations hidden behind the walls of ordinary stations. Those who believe the shepherds could never abandon their herd say the Watchers remain present, directing everyone's every move.

## The Truth

The Invisible Watchers are a remnant of the pre-war government, or [Central Metro Command](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Central_Metro_Command), having escaped to Metro-2 during [World War III](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/World_War_III). They are the "shepherds" ruling over the denizens of the Metro.

They are revealed in [Metro 2035](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2035), during the conversation between Artyom and [Aleksei Feliksovich Bessolov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Aleksei_Bessolov) in [Bessolov's bunker](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bunker_42). The truth about the Metro and its factions is laid bare: the [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line), [Hansa](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hansa), [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_(Location)), and the [Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Reich) are all puppets (or "dummies", as Bessolov puts it) controlled from behind the scenes. Wars fought between the four factions are used as a manner of "controlled conflict" — essentially, population control.

In *Metro Exodus*, during the Spartans' escape from the Metro, Miller reveals he had been directly contacted by the Invisible Watchers. They claimed to be remnants of the local Moscow government, receiving orders from the Russian federal command at [the "Ark" facility](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Ark), and maintained that the war had never ended — calling the [Spartan Order](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Spartan_Rangers) the "Moscow unit" of their Russian Special Forces, and claiming NATO forces had occupied most Russian territory. They ordered the construction and maintenance of the radio jammers, ostensibly to ensure NATO could never find the Metro.

After exploring the surface, Miller and the Rangers discovered that NATO forces had not occupied Russia and that the "Ark" had never been in contact with Moscow (having instead become a den of [cannibals](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Cannibals)). Miller concludes that the Watchers fabricated their story to manipulate the Rangers through him — the jammers were made to hide the world from Moscow, not Moscow from the world. The Watchers had manipulated all factions through disinformation, keeping them paranoid of NATO attacks or too busy fighting one another to ever realize the Watchers' control, allowing the Watchers to live in comparative luxury and security.

Upon seeing the fate of the [Novosibirsk Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Novosibirsk_Metro) — whose population was wiped out in a two-sided civil war over a dwindling medicinal resource (the [radioprotector](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Radioprotector)) — Miller comes to understand the Watchers' true purpose. By keeping themselves secret, they denied the frustrated population an easy target to rebel against. They split the Moscow Metro into four main factions ([Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza), [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_(Location)), the [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line), and the [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich)) to keep the population's attention divided, and instigated regular inter-faction conflicts to regulate the overall human population — striking a balance where the population was large enough to defend against mutants yet small enough not to overtax the Metro's limited resources. In this grim reading, the Watchers' seemingly horrendous actions had not been for their own gain alone, but had ensured that the human population as a whole survived in the Metro instead of being completely wiped out.

In the good ending of [Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus), Artyom states that the new home for him, [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Character)), and the rest of the [Aurora](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Aurora) crew is far too big for them, and that the Order is meant to save all humanity — strongly implying that Artyom and the Order are now considering finding out who hides behind the Invisible Watchers, in order to free the inhabitants of the Moscow Metro.
