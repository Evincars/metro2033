# Rangers of the Order

> "If not us, then who?"
> — Ranger motto

The **Rangers of the "SPARTA" Order** (Russian: *Орден «СПАРТА»*) are a special military order in post-apocalyptic [Russia](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Russia), operating in both the ruins of the [Moscow Metro](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moscow_Metro) and [Lake Baikal](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Lake_Baikal). A group of elite battle-hardened soldiers, they roam the wasteland, consisting of the most skilled and deadliest warriors in the whole Metro. The Rangers move individually or in small units, helping settlements as well as fighting [bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Faction)) and [mutants](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Mutants) alike, while serving as protectors of [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis).

- **Base locations:** [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis), [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)), [Sparta](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sparta_Base_(Location))
- **Leaders:** [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller) (former); [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) (determinant, Lake Baikal group)
- **Disposition towards the player:** Friendly
- **Enemies:** [Fourth Reich](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich), [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) (*Last Light*), [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza) (*Exodus*), [Dark Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones) (until D6), [Mutants](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Mutants), [Bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Faction))

## Background

The origins of the Rangers are unknown, but some of the members may have been soldiers from the Russian Army or Special Forces (Spetsnaz) before the [nuclear apocalypse](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Great_War_of_2013). It's known that they have been operating in the Metro since the Great War of 2013. The group has always been led by [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller), who is often referred to as "The Colonel", and has always been the guard and military force of Polis, though they also act independently.

Rangers seem to be greatly feared by the bandits, [Reds](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line), and [Nazis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nazi) alike. In the level "[Dry](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dry_(Level))", a conversation between bandits describes how a lone Ranger single-handedly took out large groups of men, with one bandit suggesting they might not even be human because of their fighting skills. In the first stage of *Metro: Last Light*, a Nazi officer mentions that normally the Fourth Reich doesn't interfere with the business of the Rangers.

## Bases

The Spartans maintain a large number of bases and outposts throughout the Metro. In both the *Metro 2033* book and game, their main base is Polis. However, in [Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro:_Last_Light), after the discovery of [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)) they make their main base there. The Rangers also maintain two permanent bases located in churches on the surface, both known as [Sparta](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sparta_Base_(Location)) — one near the [Smolenskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Smolenskaya) station, and the other near [Oktyabrskaya](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Oktyabrskaya) station. After the [Battle for the Bunker](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_for_D6) and during the events of 2035, the Rangers moved their permanent base to Smolenskaya station, close to Polis and to Arbat, where Miller set up his HQ.

## Technology

Throughout the Metro games, the Rangers wear a distinctive set of uniforms. In [Metro 2033](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2033_(Video_Game)), almost all Rangers wear a uniform consisting of a heavy sherpa-lined jacket, combat trousers and boots, heavy chest armor, spaulders, and the iconic Ranger helmet, usually dotted with ammunition pouches, med-kits, and knives. In the sequel and Redux, the Rangers' armor changes drastically, the only constant being their combat helmet — they use a mix of dark blue jumpsuits with armored vests and urban-camouflaged body armor.

What the Rangers lack in numbers, they make up for in technological standards, being considered the most technologically advanced faction in the whole Metro system along with Polis. Most of them were soldiers and technicians before the 2013 apocalypse, giving them an advantage in understanding equipment. Pre-war [weapons](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Weapons) are maintained by the most skilled gunsmiths the Metro can offer, favoring a [Kalash](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kalash_(AK-74M)) over a [Bastard](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bastard). Their mechanics also ensure a steady supply of well-maintained [vehicles](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Vehicles), both rail and off-road. Due to their alliance with Polis, they are also able to understand a lot of pre-war scientific knowledge, as D6 and Polis are the only two locations able to analyze the bio-weapon unleashed by the Red Line.

## Military Forces

> "For Sparta!"
> — The Rangers of the Order war-cry

The Order has approximately 100 combat-ready members, due mostly to the fact that they only take in the best of the best. This is very small, even compared to Reich standards. A frequent comparison holds that five Red soldiers are equivalent to one Ranger. It's possible the Order has more than 100 people in total, but some are dedicated specialists such as technicians, engineers, wounded Rangers, or members deployed elsewhere.

In the novel [Metro 2035](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2035), Miller states that after the battle for the bunker the Order suffered significant losses, with only 59 Rangers surviving. After secretive negotiations, [Hanza](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hanza) gave the Order dozens of potential recruits from their various special forces and elite units. Recruitment whittled them down to only 50 outstanding men. In the year 2035, the Order's full strength is 108 "including orderlies".

Tactically, the Rangers specialize in multiple uses of weaponry while keeping their infantry the strongest. The group uses more front-line rifleman tactics, though it also contains an elite sniper squad (one member being Anna) and a small demolition squad.

## Appearances

### Metro 2033

The first Ranger Artyom encounters is his step-father's friend, [Hunter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Ranger)), who has come to [Exhibition](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Exhibition_(Location)) because of reports of [telepathic mutants](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones) attacking the station. Before leaving to track the Dark Ones, Hunter tasks Artyom with delivering a message to Polis, hands him his Ranger Token (an enameled dog tag), and tells him to find Miller. He never returns and is presumed dead or missing.

Along his journey, Artyom encounters many Rangers and much evidence of their work. [Khan](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Khan_(Character)), affiliated with the Rangers, guides Artyom to the [armory](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Armory_(Level)). Later, [Pavel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Pavel_(Ranger)) and [Ulman](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ulman) save Artyom from being executed by two Nazis; Pavel does not survive the trip to Polis. At Polis, Artyom meets Miller, and after failing to gain the Council's support, they resolve to find D6. Together the Rangers and Artyom get D6 functioning enough to send a missile to the [botanical gardens](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Botanical_garden); Miller and Artyom then make their way up [Ostankino Tower](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ostankino_Tower) to launch the missiles at the Dark Ones.

### Metro: Last Light

The Rangers (now known as The Spartan Order) have changed very little. Outfitted with new armor bearing the signature "Спарта" (Sparta) on the upper back, the Rangers have occupied D6 and fortified it into their new permanent base, though they don't have full control over the facility. D6 is shown more extensively, with the Rangers' living quarters, gyms, an armory, and mess hall. While a key faction, they don't share nearly as much action as in *Metro 2033*, being featured most prominently in the [battle for D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Metro_Last_Light_Level)) against the Red Line. The game's two [endings](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Endings) decide the Rangers' fate: in the C'est la Vie ending they destroy D6 to keep the [bioweapon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Virus) from the Reds, while in the Redemption ending the [Baby Dark One](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/The_Dark_One) arrives with his [kind](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones) to help turn the tide.

## Notable Rangers

- **[Colonel Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller)** (Sviatoslav Konstantinovich Melnik) — founder and leader of the Order, a former Russian Spetsnaz officer.
- **[Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom)** — one of the younger members, recruited on the spot by Miller after helping secure D6; chosen for his natural fighting skill and marksmanship.
- **[Hunter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Ranger))** — a high-ranked Ranger considered Miller's "right hand man"; missing in action after tracking the Dark Ones.
- **[Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_Miller)** — Miller's daughter and the Order's best sniper.
- **[Ulman](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ulman)** — a higher-ranking Ranger often teamed with Artyom.
- **[Pavel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Pavel_(Ranger))** — killed while escorting Artyom through a [Nosalis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Nosalis) nest.
- **[Vladimir](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Vladimir)** — a technical expert and later quartermaster of D6.
- **[Letyaga](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Letyaga_(Ranger))** — a veteran Ranger and friend of Artyom.
- **[Idiot](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Idiot)** — field tactician, second-in-command of the Spartan Rangers aboard the Aurora in *Metro Exodus*.
- **[Sam](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sam)** — Miller's personal bodyguard, a former U.S. Marine.

## Trivia

- In the novel, [Hunter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter_(Ranger)) described his order of stalkers as "[leukocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_blood_cell)" circling around Moscow's metro system.
- Rangers are equipped with the best weapons and armor the Metro can offer; their arsenal at Sparta Base contains "the latest military equipment with outrageous costs in blood".
- Rangers are the only faction that use the [Kalash 2012](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Kalash_2012) (except in *Metro: Last Light*, where it can be found on [Red Line](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line) soldiers).
- In [Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus), Miller states the actual name of the Order is the "Joint Special Operations Forces Squadron of Moscow", the SPARTA name serving to mask their real purpose.
- It's revealed in *Metro Exodus* that the Order was formed specifically by Miller as a continuation and preservation of the Spetsnaz.
- It's confirmed in *Metro: Last Light* that there are ranks within the Order, shown when Miller addresses a Ranger as "Sergeant".
