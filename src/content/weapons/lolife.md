## Overview

The Lolife is a Metro-made semi-automatic handgun with a front-fed eight-round magazine chambered in [.44 Magnum](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/.44_Magnum). As such, the Lolife trades the power of the .44 Magnum rounds normally used for the [Revolver](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Revolver), for higher capacity and rate of fire. This generally makes what would be considered a more generous weapon in combat, allowing the missing of several shots to go by with little reprimand. It should be noted though that the Lolife's higher rate of fire results in a quicker ammo loss in prolonged firefights, and thus, it is a more expensive weapon to buy ammo for than the revolver. The magazine is mounted in front of the trigger group and it is capable of semi or fully automatic fire with the appropriate modifications.

The Lolife is commonly found with a couple of attachments when dropped by enemies, with the stock being the most common. Due to its all-around good stats, and ability to be widely customized, it is popular among many Metro-dwellers such as guards and criminals. It can also be found on many corpses in the game world.

## History

As the Lolife is a metro-made weapon, it has very little known history. It appears that Lolife has been produced since at least 2023, though not seen in the first installment of the Metro series, indicated by the pistol's popularity in the second game. The markings on the frame say "**VANO & PROF LTD. PISTOL SELF-LOADING ПАДОНОК. 2023**".

What could be ascertained would mostly be speculation as to the weapon that the Lolife was based on. The weapon shares several similarities to a few real-life firearms. Above all perhaps, the Lolife design seems to have been heavily inspired by the 1897 Bergmann No5 pistol which is very similar in appearance and also has the capability to mount a detachable stock.

## Variants and Customization

The Lolife has the most [attachments](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/attachments) available of any weapon in the game. It has 3 Optics, 2 Barrel upgrades and 4 Misc upgrades. Fully upgrading the weapon turns it from a semi-automatic pistol into a fully-automatic submachine gun with increased ammo capacity, range and accuracy compared to the stock version.

The extended magazine upgrade is not available in stores until the player reaches the [Ranger base](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sparta_Base_(Location)) in the level "[Undercity](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Undercity)". However, It can be first encountered on many enemies in the level "[Bandits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bandits_(Level))". Similarly, the autofire adapter cannot be purchased until the player reaches the merchant at the end of "[Contagion](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Contagion)". Again though it's possible to acquire one for free several levels earlier on the "[Sundown](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sundown)" mission. If the player heads to the area where the watchtower overlooks a small compound (straight ahead at the start of the mission), there is a row of small garages, one with a car blocking the door. If you look carefully, however, by crouching you can enter the building where a Lolife with an extended barrel, stock, extended magazine, autofire adapter, and laser sights is propped up against the far wall. This also counts as one of the ranger stashes towards the [Equipped](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Equipped) achievement/trophy. The autofire adapter is available in [DLC](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/DLC) mission stores and the Lolife in the "Sniper Team" level already has it equipped, essentially making it a miniature submachine gun.

The Lolife's main competition is the Revolver and Ashot. Still, a Lolife's primary advantage is its ease of use, versatile in many situations, firepower, and the wide range of attachments. However, this weapon will cost a lot of [MGR](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/MGR)s if you want to customize it. Also, you will need to scavenge for .44 Magnum rounds more often because it's easy to chew through ammo quickly. The Lolife itself is rarer and more expensive than the Revolver and Ashot. If automatic weapons are a preference, this is the weapon of choice as the sidearm. If you want to save some MGR, stick to the revolver. If you are really lacking in MGRs and cannot afford a decent shotgun, go with the Ashot.

## Trivia

- The Lolife is called the *Padonak* in the Russian dub, which literally means "Low-Life". It's also inscribed in Cyrillic markings on the receiver (ПАДОНОК). The term can be interpreted as a shiftless or immoral person ("Scumbag") or members of a despised under-class ("Chav", "Ned", or "White Trash").
- The Lolife could get its name from the fact that the weapon is commonly used by bandits and other criminals (a.k.a lowlifes). Despite this, the Lolife is not common in-game and is more expensive than the [Revolver](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Revolver) or [Ashot](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ashot), making it hard to obtain for someone with little to no MGR.
- The Lolife is based on the Kedr PP-91 (same upper frame and charging handle as the Kedr) and the OTS-02 Kiparis(Handle and barrel), with influences from other pistols such as the Mauser M712 Schnellfeuer.
- The "Vano & Prof" markings on the receiver are a reference to one of the game developers, ["Prof" Prokhorov](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Andriy_Prokhorov).
- It can be seen on Miller's side in "[Driving to Sparta](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Driving_to_Sparta)" (Redux only).
- Both [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_(Character)) and the [Red Line Sniper](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Line_Sniper) have fully customized Lolifes, indicating they are often used as secondary sub-machine guns for snipers, being compact but powerful enough to use at close range in an emergency.
- Though the Lolife is first seen in the hands of a [Nazi officer](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Fourth_Reich_Officer_(Last_Light)), it's not common among the Nazis.
- The maximum ammunition capacity of pistol rounds does not divide evenly with the magazine size of the Lolife, since the ammo cap is designed around the revolver, which has a fixed 6 round capacity. The regular magazine size of 8 does not divide evenly with the cap of 90, resulting in 11 magazines of 8 and 2 spare rounds (11x8+2). The modified magazine of 20 does not either, resulting in 4 mags of 20 and a half-mag of 10 (4x20+10). .
  - Similarly, the smaller ammunition cap of Ranger mode, 42 rounds, also does not divide evenly with the Lolife. It results in 5 magazines of 8 rounds with two left over (5x8+2), or 2 magazines of 20 rounds with 2 spare rounds (2x20+2).
- The Lolife does not appear in *[Metro Exodus](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Exodus)*, as its niche of a magazine fed .44 Magnum pistol/submachine gun is taken over by the [Bastard](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bastard), which is chambered for .44 Magnum in that game only.
  - The [Stallion](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Stallion) pistol introduced in *[Sam's Story](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Sam's_Story)* fulfils a similar niche in [Vladivostok](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Vladivostok), being a semi-automatic pistol that can be upgraded to full auto, but unlike the Lolife cannot be modified into a carbine.
