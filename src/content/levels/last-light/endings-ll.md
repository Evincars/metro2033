# Endings (Last Light)

> Attention! Spoiler ahead! This article contains major plot information.

[Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_Last_Light) has two possible endings, determined by the [moral points](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Moral_Points) the player accumulates throughout the game. The standard "bad" ending is **C'est la Vie**, in which [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) destroys [D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Location)) along with himself, while the "good" ending, **Redemption**, sees the [Dark Ones](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Dark_Ones) return to save Artyom and turn the tide of the battle.

It is possible to tell which ending will occur near the end of the [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_(Metro_Last_Light_Level)) level. As Artyom and the [Baby Dark One](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Baby_Dark_One) board the train to D6 and [Anna](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Anna_Miller) stares at Artyom, the Baby Dark One will say he will ask his kin to aid the Rangers in the battle if enough moral points have been earned — or, if not, that he must go to save his own family.

## C'est la Vie

In the standard, or "bad", ending, the Rangers are overrun by the [Red Army](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Army). [Miller](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Miller), down for the count, is approached by a gloating [Korbut](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Korbut_(Character)), who then turns to address Artyom. It is too late, however — ordered by Miller, Artyom engages D6's self-destruct system.

In the final cutscene, Artyom stands alone on the platform and closes his eyes as the explosion begins. In a tunnel vision he sees the saluting Rangers, flashes of his childhood, Anna, and the saluting faces of [Hunter](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hunter), [Uhlman](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ulman), Khan, Miller, [Alex](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Alex), and, apparently, [Bourbon](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Bourbon). His mother's words — "don't be afraid, there's nothing to be afraid of now" — echo in his head as D6 detonates. The Red Army, trapped and panicked, breaks rank as fire tears through their numbers and engulfs Metro 2 in an inferno.

The scene cuts to years later, with Anna telling the story to her and Artyom's son — how his father saved the Metro, of Artyom's bravery, and of the departure of the Dark Ones and their promised return one day.

## Redemption

In this ending, just before Artyom sets off the explosives, the Baby Dark One stops him. He and his kin — the Dark Ones trapped in D6 — save Miller, Artyom, and the surviving Rangers before turning on the Red Army, hypnotizing the soldiers into attacking one another. Korbut is personally torn apart by the little Dark One, and the Dark Ones are shown materializing among the Reds, hands reaching toward their targets.

A spinning metro-tunnel cutscene then plays over Artyom's narration of the aftermath. Uhlman is revealed to have died, "like most of the Spartans". Miller is shown in a wheelchair, having lost his feet defending D6, now in charge of the [Polis](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Polis_(Location)) guards. Khan simply disappeared after the battle. Artyom recalls Khan's words — "What if this little Dark One is the last of the angels sent to save us?" — and reflects that the little one's return with his kin may have been the pardon and salvation "for me... For the Order... For all of us in the Metro. The last light of hope in this kingdom of darkness."

Finally Artyom stands in a field with the Baby Dark One and the other Dark Ones. The Baby Dark One tells him it will be better if they go away, but that they will return one day. Artyom knows they will meet again, and believes they really were sent to save humanity: "Maybe we did earn forgiveness."

This ending is considered canon, as [Metro 2035](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro_2035) and Metro Exodus follow it.

## Trivia

- "C'est la vie" means "That's life" or "It is what it is" in French — an expression accepting that life is harsh but must be endured.
- Hunter, along with Khan, Uhlman, and [Melnik](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Melnik), can be seen giving Artyom an honor at the end of the "C'est la Vie" ending.
