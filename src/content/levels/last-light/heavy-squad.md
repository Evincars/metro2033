# Heavy Squad

Taking control of the Reich [shock trooper](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Heavy_Trooper) [Hans](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hans), accompanied by Hook and Diesel, **Heavy Squad** puts the player back at the [Frontline](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Frontline_(Location)) between the Reich and the [Red Army](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Army) sometime before or during the [Battle for D6](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Battle_for_D6). It is a short level found in the [Faction Pack DLC](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Faction_Pack_DLC) for [Metro: Last Light](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Metro:_Last_Light), consisting of a single stretch of non-stop combat, with a tremendous number of enemies attacking, lengthy sniper duels with multiple opponents firing at the same time, no consistent safe zones and an enemy encounter near the end that requires as much luck as firing accuracy. Heavy Squad has been criticized by fans for being far too difficult of a mission, even for veterans of the series, especially so on [Ranger](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ranger_Mode) [difficulties](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Difficulties).

## Overview

As a heavy weapons specialist of the Fourth Reich, Hans is particularly well-equipped for the task of holding the line. The level can be split into sections dependent on the weapon and enemy encounter Hans faces. Action begins right after the tram arrives, so there is only a little time to prepare. Players should make note of the ammo box to the left, which contains an infinite number of [medkits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Medkit) — especially since Hans only begins the level with one and will undoubtedly need more.

### The First Wave

Hans first must repel a wave of Red Army soldiers, in a manner most similar to that of which [Artyom](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Artyom) must do in the final battle of D6. Armed with a [Gatling](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Gatling), Hans must cut down around twenty soldiers in [NBC suits](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Armour#Hazard_Suit) emerging from the fire across the bridge, and roughly twenty more soldiers with red [night vision goggles](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Night_Vision_Goggles) and light armor. Standing out at the very front of the platform and holding down the fire button for constant suppression fire is not just the best offensive strategy, but a very good defensive one as well.

### The Second Wave

Hans starts the second wave when [Diesel](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Diesel) declares snipers are in the area. Moving over to the ammo box, Hans automatically arms himself with his second weapon: the [Hellbreath](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Hellbreath_(Volt_Driver)). This weapon fires powerful electro-charged [ball bearings](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Ball_Bearings) with the potential to be instant-kills, and has an equally powerful scope with impressive magnification. Remember to charge the weapon before use.

One-by-one, Hans must engage the Red Army snipers, all the while remaining wary of the others in the area. Roughly four or five can be found between the railing to the left, down the bridge, or on the other bridge to the right. Snipers are best located by their distinctive green laser-sight. Unlike the red snipers Artyom faced, these use a Valve equipped with green laser sight and can kill Hans in two to three hits, so use extreme caution while facing multiple of them. The back of the area is moderately safe for players to crouch, reload or heal.

### The Third Wave

Once again, similar to what Artyom must do at the [end](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/D6_(Metro_Last_Light_Level)) of his campaign, the Red Army attacks with a unit of shielded soldiers protecting a single Gatling unit. Like Artyom, Hans can concentrate fire on the shield unit, then take out the gatling soldier. Sniping is a much more viable option for Hans, as the Hellbreath is just as accurate and powerful as a [Preved](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Preved).

Once a shield goes down, the Gatling unit is exposed for a second or two. If not hit enough to stumble within this time-frame, the Gatling unit will begin to fire at the player — who has roughly half a second to get to cover and heal. The snipers also remain on the map, and heavy troopers will dump incendiary grenades; if you see your location light up, retreat immediately. Once the Gatling soldier is dead, snipe the remaining snipers with the Hellbreath for the final waves to appear. Diesel will offer a [Medved](https://metrovideogame.fandom.com/wiki/Medved) during this time, but taking it forces the player to drop the Hellbreath.

### The Fourth Wave

Sporting heavy armor, the next wave is similar in difficulty and method to the first. The Medved seems like the obvious choice as it is new, but can be least effective on account of its comparatively low kill rate and slow reload. The Hellbreath is viable but difficult, requiring constant headshots. Instead, the player is recommended to opt for the Gatling, as a single well-spent ammo box should be just enough to deal with the wave.

### The Fifth and Final Wave

By far the most difficult wave for some is the last. Unlike the tank faced in the main campaign's battle for D6, there is little strategy to fighting the tank that appears on the other side of the platform. Armed with a randomly firing rocket-launcher and two heavy machine guns, the tank combats the player mostly by its presence but otherwise through moving back and forth down the rail.

When the tank moves forward, the machine guns target the player and can fire through most cover. While it moves back and forth, it fires a rocket that will heavily damage or outright kill the player in its general proximity. The player's movements should be entirely reactionary, watching for the telltale sign of a rocket about to be fired — a white light at the top of the tank. The tank can be defeated with the Gatling by aiming at the top-most part where the barrel would be, or alternatively with the Medved's grenades. The tank takes around nine well-placed grenades to reduce it to its chassis, and then between one and seven more to be destroyed.

### Victory

With the tank destroyed, allies of Hans claim victory over the battle. Hans stands triumphant over a vista of corpses before him, with reinforcements from Reich coming in from behind him.

## Trivia & Bugs

- If you leave an armored soldier alive, he will still be firing at Hans in the ending cutscene, and the bullet wounds can even kill you during the cutscene, forcing you back to the start of wave 5. The achievement, however, is checked as soon as the tank is "dead", so there is no ultimate effect.
- During the loading screen, you can hear the Führer deliver speeches to his soldiers.
- Earlier screenshots showed Red Line troops rappelling from a hole in the ceiling. In the final DLC, there are no rappelling Red troops, though some can be seen briefly rappelling when Hans arrives with the railcar.
- If the player looks down once the level starts, the Gatling which Hans holds has no attachments.
